# Bank the payroll first, then lend

Jokko should launch in **Côte d'Ivoire** as a business-anchored neobank. It runs SME payroll, payouts and collections, signs up those firms' employees as retail customers, and lends to them against their salaries through a partner microfinance institution (SFD). On day one it files for its own BCEAO **payment institution (EP) licence: 100 M FCFA (≈ €152k) of capital, a 6-month statutory review and 9–15 months in practice**, and starts the slower **SFD licence** it needs for deposits and its own lending. Djamo took about three years to get its SFD licence. A WAEMU bank licence now needs **20 bn FCFA (≈ €30.5 M)**, so it is out of reach. Since May 2025 an unlicensed fintech may not touch client funds, so year one has to run on a partner bank's licence, with Jokko as the technology and brand layer.

Two rules set the economics. First, from **2 November 2026** BCEAO's PI-SPI instant-payment platform makes interoperable transfers free up to 8,000 FCFA (≈ €12) a day, so transfer fees disappear as revenue. Second, the WAEMU usury cap is **24% all-in for SFDs and 14% for banks**. That limits a 30-day loan to about **1.97% total cost**, which makes East-African-style "flash loans" illegal and confines credit to low-loss, salary-backed lending.

Senegal comes second: it is the most digitally ready market, but carries debt of about 130% of GDP and a new 0.5% transfer tax. Benin and Togo come third. Cameroon follows in year three through a bank-sponsored service on the Wave/CBC model, then Gabon, with the DRC as a separate USD-based track.

On the consumer side the market is already a fight between Wave, Djamo and Orange. On the business side only Julaya resembles Qonto, and no player combines accounts, payroll, invoicing, cards and credit. Jokko's opening is that combined product. The biggest unknowns are SME willingness to pay, which has never been measured, and several legal points on how the all-in rate is calculated. The Jokko Chain crypto project also has to be firewalled from the bank, or it becomes a licensing liability.

## Licensing: rent a bank in year one, own an EP by year two, an SFD by year three

### Partner-first is now a legal requirement

BCEAO Instruction n°001-01-2024 requires everyone providing payment services in the union to be licensed. After several extensions, the BCEAO suspended non-compliant operators from **2 May 2025** ([BIE/CCIAD](https://bie.cciad.sn/2025/05/09/regulation-des-fintechs-les-precisions-de-la-bceao/)). Sources disagree on the final cut-off. Agence Ecofin reported 1 May 2025, and later reporting gave fintechs until 31 August 2025 ([Agence Ecofin](https://www.agenceecofin.com/actualites-finance/0206-128873-services-de-paiement-umoa-les-fintech-ont-jusqu-au-31-aout-pour-se-regulariser)). Either way, Jokko cannot hold client money before it is licensed.

During that period Jokko can be the technical and brand layer for a licensed partner. Where it deals with customers, it can register as a BCEAO "agent de services de paiement". The instruction makes registration mandatory for such agents and keeps the licensed institution fully liable for them ([BCEAO Instruction 001-01-2024](https://www.bceao.int/sites/default/files/inline-files/Instruction-No001-01-2024_relative_aux_services_de_paiement_dans_l-UMOA.pdf)). All marketing must name the issuing institution ([BCEAO Instruction 008-05-2015](https://www.bceao.int/sites/default/files/2017-11/instruction_no008_05_2015_intranet.pdf)).

A helpful detail: the 2024 instruction **bans exclusivity clauses** in partnerships between payment providers. Jokko can therefore keep a second bank or rails partner in reserve.

Staying partner-only for good would cap the business. A partner bank's loans are capped at **14%**, Jokko would share economics on every line, and it would never own the customer account. The proven regional path is the one Djamo took:

1. Co-branded Visa cards issued by BGFIBank CI and Ecobank ([Djamo](https://www.djamo.com/fr-ci/carte-visa)).
2. EP licences in Côte d'Ivoire (EP.CI.005/2025) and Senegal (EP.SN.010/2025) in September 2025 ([BCEAO EP list](https://www.bceao.int/sites/default/files/2026-03/LISTE%20DES%20ETABLISSEMENTS%20DE%20PAIEMENT%20AGR%C3%89%C3%89S%20DANS%20L%E2%80%99UMOA%20au%2031%20janvier%202026.pdf)).
3. A microfinance licence for Djamo Finances SA in the same month, after **about three years** of process ([Ecofin Agency](https://www.ecofinagency.com/finance/1209-48632-ivorian-fintech-djamo-breaks-new-ground-with-bceao-microfinance-approval)).

Wave followed a similar arc. It ran on partner banks, then obtained an EME licence in April 2022 ([Financial Afrik](https://www.financialafrik.com/2022/04/21/le-pingouin-wave-mobile-money-devient-etablissement-de-monnaie-electronique/)).

#### What each licence allows and costs

**The EP is the right first vehicle.** It is the cheapest licence that lets Jokko own the customer account, run transfers, issue payment instruments and acquire card payments. It is also the entry ticket to PI-SPI.

Its limits are strict:

- It may not lend "in any manner whatsoever".
- It may not pay any interest or other benefit on balances.
- It may not use distributors, although it may use BCEAO-registered agents.
- It must ring-fence client funds at banks or microfinance institutions, with **at least 30% in sight deposits** ([BCEAO Instruction 001-01-2024](https://www.bceao.int/sites/default/files/inline-files/Instruction-No001-01-2024_relative_aux_services_de_paiement_dans_l-UMOA.pdf)).

Licence numbers are issued per country: Julaya, for example, holds separate CI, SN and BJ numbers. **In practice there is no EP passport.** The 2026 BCEAO list shows that firms which filed before the 2025 deadlines were licensed between May and September 2025. The commercial adviser Prifinance estimates **9–15 months end to end** ([Prifinance](https://prifinance.com/en/payments/cote-divoire-payment-license/)). No official application fees are published.

**The SFD licence is the only affordable route to deposits and credit.** It lends under a **24% cap** instead of the banks' 14%, which matters a great deal for short-term pricing. Its cost is time and national scope. Once deposits or loans pass **2 bn FCFA (≈ €3.05 M)**, the SFD comes under direct BCEAO and Banking Commission supervision ([Trésor CI](https://microfinance.tresor.gouv.ci/micro/bceao-et-commissions-bancaires/)).

**The EME adds little for an app-first neobank.** It costs three times as much capital as an EP, caps each customer's balance at 2 M FCFA and still cannot lend or pay interest. Its one advantage, distributor networks, matters less now that PI-SPI lets users top up from Orange Money or Wave.

#### What the notes could not confirm

- The **real SFD capital the BCEAO expects**: Djamo's figure is not public.
- **Whether one SFD can operate in several WAEMU states.**
- The **capital for a financial credit institution (EFC)**: widely quoted as 3 bn FCFA, but unverified.
- **How the BCEAO extends an EP to another country.**

The WAEMU market notes describe a "WAEMU-wide licence" that opens eight markets. The primary BCEAO texts contradict this: an EME needs a separate authorisation per country (art. 20), and EP licences are numbered per country. **Plan on one licence per country.**

### CEMAC and DRC entry modes

In CEMAC, a fintech can enter on a partner bank's licence. COBAC decision D-2025/122 (11 June 2025) authorised **Commercial Bank Cameroun to offer a "Wave" service**: cash-in/out, P2P, merchant and bill payments, and inbound international transfers. The BEAC first issued a no-objection on the technology, and the funds sit at CBC ([Droit Médias Finance](https://droitmediasfinance.com/index.php/actualites/droit-tech-fintech/1022-cameroun-la-cobac-accorde-lautorisation-prealable-a-commercial-bank-cameroun-pour-son-nouveau-service-de-paiement-wave)). Cameroon's Ministry of Finance also gave fintechs a three-month ultimatum in 2025 to get licensed ([Launch Base Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2025/05/16/cameroons-fintech-ultimatum-three-months-to-license-or-cease-operations/?amp=1)).

CEMAC payment-institution rules are being redrafted, and the sources point in opposite directions:

- One draft would **double EP capital to 1 bn FCFA**.
- The June 2026 Libreville consultations agreed a **lower** minimum for "second-category" payment operators, easier use of distributors and a future sandbox, without figures ([Sika Finance](https://www.sikafinance.com/marches/cemac-la-cobac-affine-les-futures-regles-des-services-bancaires-et-des-paiements_62676)).

**Treat CEMAC capital as unknown until COBAC adopts the texts.**

In the DRC, the **USD 2.5 M e-money capital floor** is about five times the WAEMU EME floor ([Maxence Kiyana](https://maxencekiyana.com/la-monnaie-electronique-en-rdc/)). A bank partnership is the only capital-efficient way in.

### Firewall Jokko Chain before the first filing

Regulators in the region are hostile to crypto or uninterested in it:

- In July 2026 the BCEAO Governor said crypto-assets "are not a currency and are not regulated", and no licensing framework exists ([Ecofin](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-finances/2407-57691-waemu-cryptoasset-regulation-remains-in-preparation-bceao-urges-caution)).
- In August 2026 Guinea's central bank ordered e-money and payment providers to block and report wallets used in crypto scams ([Droit Médias Finance](https://droitmediasfinance.com/index.php/actualites/droit-tech-fintech/1362-guinee-cryptoactifs-la-banque-centrale-de-la-republique-de-guinee-bcrg-interdit-lusage-des-cryptomonnaies-comme-monnaie-ayant-cours-legal)).
- Djamo publicly rules out crypto ([ITWeb](https://itweb.africa/article/djamo-targets-francophone-africa-with-40m-raise/nWJad7bNlr47bjO1)).

Licensing reviews look at the people behind an applicant. Even the BCEAO's registration of payment agents examines the fitness of their managers. So a shared name with a crypto project is a real risk in the file.

The firewall should include:

- **A separate legal entity and holding line** for the neobank.
- **No shared treasury, wallets or customer data.**
- **No stablecoin or crypto rails anywhere in the stack**, including card programmes from crypto-adjacent issuers such as Stripe's Bridge ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/newsroom/news/bridge-partners-with-visa)).
- **Proactive disclosure** of the founders' other venture in the licence file.
- **Serious consideration of a distinct brand**, because "Jokko" alone will be searched by examiners and customers.

Whether the shared brand is acceptable is a question for counsel, listed below.

#### What the WAEMU data shows

The striking pattern is that **WAEMU mobile money is huge but shallow**:

- 248.7 M registered accounts, of which only 76.9 M (30.9%) were active in 2024.
- 160,415 bn FCFA (≈ €244.6 bn) of flows ([BCEAO digital financial services report 2024](https://www.bceao.int/sites/default/files/2026-03/Rapport%20annuel%20sur%20les%20services%20financiers%20num%C3%A9riques%20dans%20l'UEMOA%20-%202024.pdf)).
- About 88% of wallet deposits are later withdrawn as cash ([Launch Base Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2026/03/24/the-267bn-crown-wave-edges-closer-to-dethroning-orange-in-west-africa/)).
- **E-money issuers as a group lost 17.3 bn FCFA (≈ €26 M) in 2024 on 291.3 bn FCFA (≈ €444 M) of revenue** ([CB-UMOA key figures](https://www.bceao.int/sites/default/files/inline-files/Chiffres_cl%C3%A9s_du_Rapport_annuel_2024_de_la_Commission_Bancaire.pdf)).

Together these show that moving money is not a business on its own. The opportunity lies in keeping money digital (salary, savings, cards, merchant payments) and in credit.

**Côte d'Ivoire** has the scale that matters for a business-anchored model:

- 69% of the union's active merchants.
- About 4 M wage earners, of whom roughly 10% of adults are still paid only in cash. That is a payroll-digitisation opportunity.
- Moderate debt and 6%+ growth.
- A 2026 tax rule that makes business payments by mobile money deductible ([KPMG CI](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/ci/pdf/newsletter/mensuel-juridique-012026.pdf.coredownload.inline.pdf)).
- A **mandatory electronic invoicing regime (FNE)** that pushed SMEs onto API-based invoicing between 2025 and February 2026 ([Sage](https://www.sage.com/fr-ma/blog/la-fne-en-cote-divoire-guide-complet-sage-cote-divoire/)).
- The best eKYC coverage: Smile ID claims connections to two ID authorities covering 10 M+ records ([Smile ID](https://usesmileid.com/countries/cote-d-ivoire)).

**Senegal** is the most ready on demand: 76.5% of adults hold an account, 60.8% have a smartphone as their main phone, and **32% of adults are paid digitally**. It is also the region's diaspora hub, with **2,211 bn FCFA (≈ €3.37 bn)** of remittances in 2024 ([Launch Base Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2026/03/19/bceao-opens-a-new-playbook-for-west-african-fintechs-in-diaspora-banking/)).

But Senegal's headwinds are real:

- The IMF found the 2023 deficit was 12.3% of GDP, not the 4.9% reported. A US$2.2 bn programme is still awaiting Board approval ([France 24](https://www.france24.com/en/live-news/20260901-imf-reaches-agreement-with-senegal-on-new-2-2-bn-loan-programme)).
- Growth is projected at 2.2% for 2026.
- Law 2025-17 imposes **0.5% per transfer, capped at 2,000 FCFA (≈ €3.05), plus 0.5% withholding on merchant receipts**, while exempting salary payments and ordinary bank transfers ([Seneweb](https://www.seneweb.com/en/news/Societe/orange-money-la-taxe-sur-les-transferts-dargent-tta-de-05-entre-en-vigueur-des-le-17-decembre_n_477669.html)).

That exemption tilts the field toward bank-account-based salary products, which is good for Jokko's model. Austerity, however, raises risk on public-sector salary advances.

#### What the CEMAC and DRC data shows

**CEMAC's problem is macro and FX, not demand.** Reserves fell to **4.2 months of imports in 2025**, below the zone's 5-month benchmark, against **7.8 months in WAEMU** ([World Bank CEMAC Barometer](https://www.worldbank.org/en/region/afr/publication/economic-barometer-for-the-central-african-economic-and-monetary-community-summer-2026); [IMF](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/05/21/pr26166-imf-concludes-2026-discussions-on-common-policies-of-waemu)). The BEAC denies any devaluation, but in May 2026 Citigroup called it "the core of the CEMAC economic story" ([Finance Week](https://financeweek.net/blog/cemac-citi-preconise-une-devaluation-du-franc-cfa-apres-loffensive-de-la-beac-sur-le-rapatriement-des-devises)).

The FX rules also undercut the "multi-currency" promise. Residents may not hold foreign-currency accounts without BEAC authorisation, and cards are limited to 5 M FCFA (≈ €7,622) per trip abroad and 1 M FCFA (≈ €1,524) per month online ([Agence Ecofin](https://www.agenceecofin.com/finances-publiques/2806-67370-la-beac-plafonne-les-retraits-et-paiements-par-carte-bancaire-hors-de-la-cemac)).

On top of that, Cameroon is on the FATF grey list and taxes mobile money at **0.2% plus 4 FCFA per transaction** ([Business in Cameroon](https://www.businessincameroon.com/finance/1412-15500-cameroon-mobile-money-tax-sharply-raises-transaction-costs-imf-warns)).

Cameroon is still the right CEMAC beachhead:

- About 444,000 formal enterprises ([Journal du Cameroun](https://fr.journalducameroun.com/cameroun-voici-les-chiffres-des-pme-en-2024/)).
- 65% of CEMAC mobile money accounts.
- No neobank-grade competitor. Wave entered only in 2025, under CBC's licence.

**Gabon** is rich per head and has a near-monopoly bank charging 21% on average. It is also tiny, and its sovereign is rated CCC-.

**The DRC** is the largest raw market: 114 M people and about 34 M active wallets. It has the best fit for multi-currency, because the economy is USD-based. Its risks are low incomes, the war in the east and a de-dollarisation drive that bans foreign-currency cash transactions from April 2027.

#### Where the business gaps are

**No player combines** an instantly opened account with its own RIB, multi-operator payroll with approvals, FNE-compliant invoicing with reconciliation, international-ready expense cards and data-driven working-capital credit.

The pan-African evidence says that last piece, credit on proprietary data, decides who wins:

- **Moniepoint** lent more than ₦1 tn (~$720 M) to SMEs in a year, underwritten on its own payment data ([Launch Base Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2026/01/29/from-payments-to-credit-nigerian-fintech-moniepoint-deploys-720m-to-corner-shops-and-supermarkets)).
- **Brass**, an account-only SME player with no credit engine and no balance-sheet strength, ended up absorbed into Paystack's microfinance bank in 2026 ([Brand Spur](https://brandspurng.com/2026/06/30/brass-to-shut-down-as-independent-fintech-customers-move-to-paystack-microfinance-bank/)).

## Start with employers, then bank their employees

The evidence points to a **B2B-anchored, B2C-enabled** entry, with four arguments behind it.

**First, both consumer wedges that worked from 2020 to 2024 are being commoditised.**

- Free transfers are now a regulatory baseline under PI-SPI.
- Virtual cards are free from Wave and arriving from every telco. Commentators now describe prepaid cards as good for acquisition but low-margin ([Launch Base Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2025/09/12/facing-a-telco-onslaught-djamos-answer-is-now-a-banking-license-and-a-new-model)).
- Djamo's own response to the telco onslaught was to become a lender. It did not double down on cards.

**Second, payroll is the cheapest route to good consumers.**

- Employees paid into Jokko arrive with a salary stream Jokko can see and sweep. That is the precondition for low-loss credit under a 24% cap.
- Djamo gets only 5–10% of users' salaries ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/03/djamo-raises-17m-and-has-1m-users-across-francophone-africa)).
- In CI about 10% of adults are still paid only in cash, and in Togo the share is the same, against 4–6% paid into a bank ([Global Findex 2025](https://www.worldbank.org/en/publication/globalfindex)).

**Third, business tooling on PI-SPI is under-served.** The API-Business tier listed only **24 approved names** in mid-September 2026, and analysts expect SME treasury, payroll and reconciliation tools to come first ([Financial Afrik](https://www.financialafrik.com/2026/10/04/la-bceao-rend-pi-spi-obligatoire-revoit-ses-tarifs-et-pose-enfin-la-question-de-nouveaux-services-financiers-adosses-a-la-plateforme/)).

**Fourth, an EP cannot lend or pay interest.** In the 12–24 months before the SFD arrives, Jokko needs revenue that does not depend on credit. Business subscriptions and payout fees can provide it. Consumer interchange alone is thin.

**The case for consumers first deserves a fair hearing.** Djamo reached a million consumers with 25% paying for Premium, which proves consumers in CI will pay for cards and international features. Consumers also bring the deposits that will later fund lending.

The business-first route has real costs:

- Sales cycles are slower.
- Business onboarding (KYB) is manual: CI requires a signatory to be physically present, and Djamo requires the legal representative at signup ([Djamo](https://www.djamo.com/en-ci/business-account)).
- **No SME willingness-to-pay survey exists.**

The recommendation therefore keeps a **self-serve consumer app open from launch**, free for employees of client firms and with a Premium tier. It **holds paid consumer acquisition until salary advance and savings are live**, because a consumer without credit or savings is an unprofitable customer of a free transfer rail.

**What employers get:**

- Payroll, supplier payouts and collections across every wallet and bank via PI-SPI.
- Approvals and audit trails.
- FNE-compliant invoices tied to payment links and automatic reconciliation.
- Employee cards.

**What employees get:**

- A free salary account with savings pots and an international card.
- **Salary advance through a partner SFD.**

**What sits around the core:**

- **Option D** is the Premium tier from launch: freelancers and agency owners are the bridge segment between consumer and business.

### What is deliberately left out of the MVP

**Resident EUR/USD balances.** In WAEMU, Règlement 06/2024/CM/UEMOA requires the Finance Minister's prior authorisation, on the BCEAO's concurring opinion, for residents to open foreign-currency accounts ([Règlement 06/2024](https://www.labase-lextenso.fr/sites/lextenso/files/lextenso_upload/l_regl._ndeg_062024cmuemoa.pdf)). CEMAC Règlement 02/18 bans them by default ([droit-afrique](https://www.droit-afrique.com/uploads/CEMAC-Reglement-2018-02-changes.pdf)). "Multi-currency" at launch therefore means an FCFA account, good FX at the moment of card spend, and inbound foreign income converted on receipt. Holding foreign currency comes later through an authorised bank partner. In the DRC, by contrast, USD/CDF accounts are table stakes.

**Interest on savings pots.** An EP may not pay interest. Interest arrives with Jokko's own SFD, or earlier if counsel confirms Jokko can distribute a partner SFD's savings product.

**East-African-style flash loans, pre-approved credit and anything crypto.**

**Agent cash networks.** EPs may not use distributors. Cash top-ups ride on Orange and Wave through PI-SPI. Djamo's field agents for onboarding are a model worth copying for business KYB, under BCEAO agent registration.

### Card design

The MVP card is a locally issued WAEMU BIN through a partner bank, run by an issuer-processor connected to GIM-UEMOA, Visa and Mastercard. Paymentology claims all three connections ([Paymentology](https://www.paymentology.com/demo-africa)). Djamo's co-founder says merchants' fraud models approve local BINs more readily ([Launch Base Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2024/11/20/in-francophone-west-africa-payment-cards-are-run-by-banks-not-fintechs/)).

The card exists for parity, not as the main differentiator. **Differentiation comes from approval rates on Meta, Google, Apple and airlines, transparent FX and dispute handling.** Whether a debit card tied to an EP account, rather than a prepaid card, would clear Apple's exclusion is unverified.

## A 24% all-in cap turns flash loans into salary-backed credit

### What is legal

In WAEMU only banks, financial credit institutions and SFDs may lend. EMEs and EPs cannot. Decision n°19/29-12-2025/CM/UMOA, in force from **1 June 2026**, caps the all-in annual rate (TEG/TAEG) at **14% for banks and 24% for SFDs and financial credit institutions**. The all-in rate includes "the whole of fees, commissions and insurance premiums linked to granting the credit", whatever the contract form ([BCEAO decision](https://www.bceao.int/sites/default/files/2026-01/Decision-No19-du-29-12-2025_CMUMOA_fixant_le_taux_de_l-usure.pdf); [Financial Afrik](https://www.financialafrik.com/2026/07/27/taux-dusure-dans-luemoa-le-recalibrage-opere-par-la-decision-n19-29-12-2025-cm-umoa/)). The same commentary explicitly flags ancillary fees kept outside the all-in rate as a circumvention risk.

Penalties are:

- fines, and imprisonment for repeat offences;
- voiding of the clause or the whole contract, with excess amounts refunded;
- Banking Commission sanctions up to licence withdrawal.

Sources disagree on whether the SFD cap was previously 27%. The current **24% is consistent across every source**.

| Lender of record | Annual cap (all-in) | Max total cost, 7-day loan | Max total cost, 30-day loan | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SFD or financial credit institution | 24% | 0.46% | **1.97%** | 25,000 FCFA (≈ €38) for 30 days: ≤ ~490 FCFA (≈ €0.75) all-in. 10,000 FCFA (≈ €15) for 7 days: ≤ ~46 FCFA (≈ €0.07) |
| Bank | 14% | 0.27% | 1.15% | 100,000 FCFA (≈ €152) for 30 days: ≤ ~1,151 FCFA (≈ €1.75) |
| CEMAC lender | Set per country and quarter by the BEAC monetary policy committee | Varies | Varies | Average all-in rate 11.8% (Q2 2025); SME rate 23.3% |

Calculations are pro-rata on the annual cap. CEMAC data: [droitmediasfinance](https://droitmediasfinance.com/index.php/dmf-conseils/protection-du-consommateur-financier/1284-cemac-financement-par-credits-comment-se-determine-le-taux-dusure-en-zone-cemac); [Gabon Media Time](https://gabonmediatime.com/cemac-les-pme-penalisees-par-des-couts-de-credit-de-plus-en-plus-eleves/).

**This arithmetic settles the "flash loan" question.**

East African nano-lending runs on fees of 7.5–15% a month:

- M-Shwari charges a 7.5% facilitation fee per 30-day loan ([Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/companies/m-shwari-loan-charge-not-interest-cba-tells-customers-2126276)).
- The smallest Kenyan digital loans default at **83.1%** below KES 1,000 ([Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/economy/borrowers-fail-to-repay-83pc-of-digital-loans-below-sh1000-5189888)).

Under a 24% cap, a 7-day nano-loan earns at most 0.46%. That cannot cover any meaningful loss rate, let alone acquisition and collection costs.

Two local data points do not fit this picture:

- **MTN's MoMoKash in Benin advertises fees of 9–14% for 7–30 days** ([MTN Benin FAQ](https://www.mtn.bj/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/FAQ-MOMOKASH.pdf)). That is hard to reconcile with the cap, and how operators structure it legally is unknown. **Jokko should not copy competitor fee grids.**
- Djamo's reported loan pricing, **0.78% interest plus a 1% fee**, works out to about 1.78% a month. If the tenor is 30 days, that sits just inside the 24% cap. It comes from a low-reliability comparison site ([Erooamba](https://erooamba.com/posts/djamo-vs-wave-cote-ivoire-2026)), but it is the pricing pattern a compliant lender would use.

### Partner structure

The EME instruction lets a bank or SFD loan be credited to a wallet, which is the legal basis for distributing credit ([BCEAO Instruction 008-05-2015](https://www.bceao.int/sites/default/files/2017-11/instruction_no008_05_2015_intranet.pdf)). Jokko should partner with an **SFD or financial credit institution, not a bank**. The SFD cap gives 10 points more pricing headroom.

Candidates named in the notes:

- **Baobab:** loan book of €963 M (~631.7 bn FCFA), PAR30 2.06% at end-2025 ([Baobab](https://baobab.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Kit-Presse_2026_800x600_FR.pdf)).
- **Cofina:** partner in the Orange/Yabx and Yango driver-loan programmes ([Connection Ivoirienne](https://connectionivoirienne.net/2024/12/10/en-cote-divoire-yango-sassocie-a-yabx-et-cofina-pour-lancer-des-services-de-pret-numerique-integres/)).
- **Advans**, and **PAMECAS** in Senegal, which also has an Islamic window.

How responsibilities split:

- **The partner** owns the credit decision, the balance sheet, the contract, all-in rate disclosure and credit-bureau reporting.
- **Jokko** owns the user experience, data, scoring model and collections, and provides first-loss cover.

The commonly cited **10–20% first-loss** figure is an industry assumption, not a sourced norm. It should be negotiated.

The regulatory direction makes this clean split matter. In August 2026 the BCEAO was debating whether telcos and fintechs that lend should face bank-equivalent rules or a new "digital bank" category ([Senego](https://senego.com/bceao-telecoms-et-fintechs-avancent-vers-le-credit-le-cadre-prudentiel-en-debat_1998398.html)).

### Credit bureaus

WAEMU's BIC (run by Creditinfo West Africa) holds over 40 M contracts on **18 M individuals from 318 institutions**. There is no evidence it receives mobile money data ([droitmediasfinance](https://droitmediasfinance.com/index.php/actualites/droit-bancaire/1123-uemoa-bureau-dinformation-sur-le-credit-bilan-dune-decennie-de-transformation-et-perspectives-a-lere-du-digital)). Jokko's scoring edge is therefore **first-party salary and payment data**. The bureau serves as a negative check.

### Conduct standard

Adopt the Kenyan and Nigerian conduct standards now. Nigeria's 2025 digital-lending rules (DEON) ban pre-authorised or automatic lending and unethical marketing, with fines of up to ₦100 M or 1% of turnover ([FCCPC](https://www.fccpc.gov.ng/media/press-releases/digital-lending-fccpc-tackles-abuses-issues-landmark-regulations/)). ## Free transfers push revenue into subscriptions, cards, float and credit

### What PI-SPI changes

From 2 November 2026, interoperable e-money transfers in WAEMU must go through PI-SPI. Sending is **free up to 8,000 FCFA (≈ €12) a day per user per provider**, which the BCEAO says covers about 75% of transactions. Above that the fee is at most 0.8%, and receiving is always free ([Financial Afrik](https://www.financialafrik.com/2026/10/04/la-bceao-rend-pi-spi-obligatoire-revoit-ses-tarifs-et-pose-enfin-la-question-de-nouveaux-services-financiers-adosses-a-la-plateforme/)). Withdrawals, merchant payments, bills, salary disbursements and credit remain freely priced ([Senego](https://senego.com/bceao-les-petits-transferts-gratuits-mais-les-retraits-restent-hors-plafond_2009714.html)). Whether the 0.8% applies to the whole amount or only the excess above 8,000 FCFA is **not yet clear**.

The implication: the **retail P2P line, Wave's 1% and Djamo's 1.5%, is gone for new entrants**. But PI-SPI also removes Wave's and Orange's network moat, because a Jokko user can now pay anyone by phone-number alias.

### Two illustrations

**A salary advance.** All figures are calculated, not sourced.

- A 100,000 FCFA (≈ €152), 30-day advance can earn at most **1,973 FCFA (≈ €3.01)** all-in.
- Funding at an assumed 8% a year costs **658 FCFA (≈ €1.00)**.
- That leaves about **1,315 FCFA (≈ €2.00)** to cover losses, servicing and margin.
- A **1% loss per cycle (1,000 FCFA) would consume three quarters of it.**

The product works only with **near-zero losses**: salary domiciliation, an employer's deduction mandate, and microfinance-quality books like Baobab's 2.06% PAR30 ([Baobab](https://baobab.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Kit-Presse_2026_800x600_FR.pdf)). It also works only with high reuse, because annualised the line earns about 24% on capital deployed against funding costs of 7–9%.

**An SME client.** The 150,000 FCFA average net salary is my assumption.

- A 40-employee firm paying that salary moves **6 M FCFA (≈ €9,147)** of payroll a month.
- At Wave's 1% anchor, that payroll is worth **60,000 FCFA (≈ €91) a month in payout fees**. That figure is Jokko's price ceiling for payroll plus subscription combined.
- Each such client also brings 40 salary accounts at almost no acquisition cost, and each is a potential salary-advance borrower.

### Benchmarks to plan against

Djamo's implied revenue per user is **$10–50 a year** (Dealroom revenue estimate divided by about 1 M users), against Nubank's roughly $15 a month ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/companies/djamo/); [Nu Holdings](https://international.nubank.com.br/company/nu-holdings-ltd-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025-financial-results/)). Retail revenue per user will be single or low double-digit dollars. Business revenue per client must carry the model until credit scales.

### Taxes

Taxes add friction, and the sources need reconciling:

- CI taxes 7.2% of mobile money transfer revenue, borne by operators ([Agence Ecofin](https://www.agenceecofin.com/fiscalite/2602-64267-cote-divoire-la-taxe-sur-le-mobile-money-est-desormais-effective)).
- A commercial adviser cites a **5% tax on monthly money-transfer turnover under article 1130 of the tax code** ([Prifinance](https://prifinance.com/en/payments/cote-divoire-payment-license/)).
- VAT is 18%.

A tax adviser must reconcile these figures.

## Conclusion

Regulators have already done the disrupting in francophone West Africa, and they did it in an unusual direction. PI-SPI made transfers free, and the 24% all-in cap made expensive nano-credit illegal. Together they reverse the East African playbook that most "flash loan" pitches assume. The moat is no longer distribution or price, which Wave already owns. It is two scarcer things: **licences that take years to obtain** and **low-loss data about where people's salaries land**.

A Qonto-style SME tool and a payroll-linked consumer bank are therefore not two strategies to choose between. They are one flywheel. The business account supplies the salary data that makes credit legal and profitable, and credit supplies the margin that the free transfer rail cannot. The practical consequence is that Jokko's real critical path is the SFD file, not the app. Every month the SFD application is not filed is a month added to the date Jokko can lend on its own balance sheet. The second critical item is the Jokko Chain firewall, which costs little now and a great deal if an examiner finds the link first.
