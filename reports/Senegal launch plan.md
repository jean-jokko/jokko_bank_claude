# Senegal first: B2B now, B2C in years 2–3

This plan adapts the regional strategy to a **Senegal-first launch**, chosen because the team is in Dakar. Côte d'Ivoire follows as the second market. The model stays the same: sell payroll and payouts to employers, bank their employees, then lend to those employees against their salaries through a partner microfinance institution (SFD). A broad consumer push comes in years 2–3, built on that base.

Senegal scored below Côte d'Ivoire in the regional scorecard (3.50 vs 4.30). Most of that gap comes from macro risk and a smaller SME base, not from demand. Having the team on the ground narrows it: regulator meetings, employer sales and partner negotiations all happen in person here. Detailed sources are in the research notes (`senegal_deep_dive.md`). Amounts use 1 EUR = 655.957 FCFA.

## Senegal in ten facts

| # | Fact | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **30.5% of adults earn wages; 12% are paid into a bank and 20.1% by phone; only 7.5% are paid in cash only** (Findex 2025) | Salaries are already digital. Jokko competes to become the account salaries land in, not to digitise cash |
| 2 | **Salary payments are exempt from the 0.5% transfer tax** (Law 2025-17); supplier payouts and merchant receipts are taxed at 0.5% | Payroll is the one untaxed business flow, which favours a payroll-led product |
| 3 | **IPRES counts 577,171 contributing employees and 40,300 contributing employers (2024)**; the State pays about 195,000 agents | Formal payroll pool of about 0.75–0.8M people. That is the B2B addressable base |
| 4 | Formal modern-sector pay averages **about 380,000 FCFA (≈ €579) a month** (calculated from ANSD data); minimum wage 64,223 FCFA (≈ €98) | Salary advances of 50,000–150,000 FCFA are realistic tickets |
| 5 | About **26,000 companies a year** register through APIX; the 2016 census found 97% of businesses informal and only ~18,000 firms filing accounts | The formal SME market is small and concentrated in Dakar, so sales can be targeted |
| 6 | **Wave holds about 80% of e-money balances** (459 bn of 571 bn FCFA, Aug 2026) and charges 1% (paid by the sender) on bulk payouts | Wave is the price anchor and the default wallet, so Jokko must interoperate with it, not fight it |
| 7 | **PI-SPI becomes mandatory on 2 Nov 2026**: free transfers up to 8,000 FCFA a day; 43 participants in Senegal | Any user can pay any wallet or bank, so a new account no longer starts with a network handicap |
| 8 | **E-invoicing is legislated, but the tax authority has published no rollout calendar** (penalty: 25% of VAT, up to 5 M FCFA per invoice) | Unlike Côte d'Ivoire, invoicing is not an urgent pain yet. It is a roadmap item in Senegal, not the launch hook |
| 9 | Public debt **~130% of GDP**, 2026 growth forecast at 2.2%; the US$2.2 bn IMF programme is still awaiting Board approval | Macro risk: State arrears to SMEs, possible new taxes, austerity pressure on civil-servant pay |
| 10 | Remittances of **2,211 bn FCFA (≈ €3.37 bn) in 2024**; Senegal receives 64% of inbound international mobile money in WAEMU; a BCEAO notice (13 Mar 2026) lets the diaspora open accounts remotely | Strongest diaspora market in the region: a key B2C angle for years 2–3 |

## Part 1: Senegal B2B (launch)

### Who to sell to

| Segment | Size signal | What they need | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Formal employers with 10–500 staff, Dakar** (services, distribution, schools, clinics, BTP, agencies, NGOs) | Part of the 40,300 IPRES-paying employers | Payroll to any bank or wallet with approvals, payslips, IPRES/CSS-ready exports, supplier payouts, cards for staff spending | **1** |
| **NGOs, projects and mass-payout organisations** (cash transfers, field staff, per diems) | Many donor-funded programmes in Senegal | Bulk payouts to thousands of wallets, audit trails, reconciliation | **1** (fast volume, simple product) |
| **Their employees** | About 14 per IPRES employer on average (577k ÷ 40.3k); target clients have more | A salary account with an IBAN, a card that works online, a salary advance | **1** (comes with each employer) |
| Freelancers, agencies, online sellers | Not quantified | International card, getting paid from abroad | 2 (Premium tier) |
| Informal micro-merchants | Large, but Wave and Orange dominate | QR acceptance | 3 (later) |

### What to launch with

| For employers | For employees |
|---|---|
| Business account on the partner bank's rails, then on Jokko's own EP account | Free salary account with Jokko bank details (RIB/IBAN) |
| **Payroll and bulk payouts to every wallet and bank** (Wave, Orange Money, Mixx, Free, banks) via PI-SPI | Virtual card (local BIN, Visa or Mastercard), then physical card |
| Roles, approval chains and an audit trail | Transfers, bill pay, savings vaults (no interest until Jokko has its SFD) |
| Payslips and IPRES/CSS exports | **Salary advance through a partner SFD** (target: 6 months after launch) |
| Supplier payouts and payment links | Clear fees, anti-scam confirmations, real customer support |
| Expense cards for staff | |

Leave out of the Senegal launch: e-invoicing (no deadline yet), merchant acceptance, foreign-currency accounts (residents need ministerial approval), and anything crypto.

### Competitors in Senegal and the gap

| Player | Strength | Gap Jokko can use |
|---|---|---|
| **Wave / Wave Business** | ~80% of e-money balances, 1% bulk payouts, the default wallet | Not a bank account: no IBAN, no approvals or payroll workflow, no credit yet |
| **Julaya SN** (EP licence) | Only real business workflow tool: approvals, payroll, its own RIB | No employee accounts, no salary advance, no published pricing |
| **Djamo SN** (EP licence) | Zero-fee business account, consumer brand, well funded | Consumer-born; light business workflows; only 5–10% of its users get their salary there |
| **Orange Bank Africa** | Leader in digital credit (Tik Tak); grew from 11 to 557k Senegal accounts in 2025 | Tiny loans only (up to 1 M FCFA); no payroll product |
| PayDunya, Bictorys, Flutterwave SN, Naboopay | Online collections | Collections only, no accounts |
| Banks | Full accounts, credibility | In-person onboarding taking days, 10,000–25,000 FCFA a month in fees, poor digital tools |
| Fintra (salary advance, 2022) | First mover | Pilot appears dormant; its pricing (6% for 15 days) would breach the rate cap if treated as credit |

**The open gap: nobody combines payroll with approvals, IPRES-ready exports, employee accounts with an IBAN, and a compliant salary advance.** Julaya pays your people but does not bank or lend to them; Djamo banks consumers but does not run your payroll; Wave is cheap but is not a bank account.

### Pricing anchor (illustrative, to be tested)

A 40-employee Dakar company at the average formal salary moves about **15.2 M FCFA (≈ €23,200) of payroll a month**. At Wave's 1% that is **152,000 FCFA (≈ €232) a month**. That is the ceiling for Jokko's payroll fee plus subscription combined. Undercutting it with a flat monthly price per company (for example 25,000–75,000 FCFA by size) is a strong sales argument. These price points are hypotheses; an SME survey in Dakar must test them before launch.

### Salary advance: what Senegalese law allows

- **Lender of record:** a partner SFD, priced within the **24% all-in annual cap** (about **2% total cost for 30 days**). Banks are capped at 14%.
- **Repayment:** salary domiciliation in Jokko plus an employer agreement to route pay through Jokko. A formal salary assignment requires a declaration before a court president (Code de procédure civile art. 571-2), which does not work at scale.
- **Deduction limits:** art. 381 sets the seizable share at 10%, 20%, 30% and 40% across bands up to 200,000 FCFA a month, and fully seizable above that, with all deductions capped at two-thirds of net pay. **A 2023 law-firm note says art. 381 was abrogated; counsel must confirm.**
- **A possible shortcut:** Labour Code art. L.130 says wage advances on pay already earned ("acomptes") are not loans. An employer-funded earned-wage advance could therefore sit outside credit rules. **Counsel must confirm before launch.**

### Senegal B2B risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Sovereign debt crisis, State arrears to SMEs, austerity | Focus on private employers and NGOs; no civil-servant salary advance until the IMF programme is approved and stable |
| New taxes on digital flows | Lean on the salary exemption; keep pricing flat rather than per-transaction |
| Wave launches credit and savings | Compete on payroll workflow and employee banking, where Wave has nothing |
| Rate-cap breach through fees | Written counsel opinion; all fees inside the all-in rate |
| Licence delays | File the EP early, with a complete file; keep partner rails as fallback |
| Small formal market | Plan the move into Côte d'Ivoire at months 12–18 |

## Part 2: Senegal B2C in years 2–3

### What will have changed by then

- Person-to-person transfers will be almost free everywhere, including across WAEMU from **1 June 2027**.
- Wave will probably have launched credit and savings; Orange Bank Africa will keep growing; Djamo will be better funded.
- The BCEAO may have created a "digital bank" licence category (under debate in 2026).
- A national e-ID is possible under the government's digital plan, but has no date.
- Jokko should hold its Senegal EP and be close to, or holding, its own SFD licence.

### How to enter consumers then

| Angle | Why it works in Senegal | Depends on |
|---|---|---|
| **Convert the salary-account base** | Every employer client brings employees at near-zero acquisition cost | B2B traction in years 1–2 |
| **Primary account with a card that works everywhere** | 32.7% of adults already hold a debit card, the highest in the region; prepaid cards are refused by Apple and some online services | Card sponsor and approval-rate testing |
| **Salary-backed instalment loans** (larger than nano-loans) | Tik Tak and Djamo cap at 1 M FCFA; salaried users want more | Own SFD or a strong SFD partner |
| **Diaspora family account** | 2,211 bn FCFA of remittances; remote account opening allowed since March 2026 | A European collection partner; legal clarity on non-resident accounts |
| **Islamic account and Murabaha financing** | Banque Islamique du Sénégal is now #3 by balance sheet, with a US$40 M IFC Murabaha line; PAMECAS has an Islamic window | Islamic SFD or bank partner |
| **Savings with a real yield** | Tontines remain the main savings tool | Own SFD (an EP cannot pay interest) |

## Part 3: What a bank will ask you for

Banks look at a fintech partnership through four lenses: **regulatory risk** (will the BCEAO blame us?), **AML risk**, **operational and IT risk**, and **commercial value** (deposits, card volume, new SME clients). Prepare a data room that answers all four before the first meeting.

### 1. The company file

- Statutes, company registration (RCCM), tax ID (NINEA), tax and IPRES/CSS clearance certificates.
- Audited or management accounts, cap table, and proof of funding (runway of at least 18–24 months).
- **Beneficial-owner declaration** with politically-exposed-person and sanctions screening of every shareholder and director.
- CVs of the management team, especially the compliance officer and CTO.
- A 3-year business plan with volumes (accounts, transactions, card spend, balances).

**About Jokko Chain:** the bank will screen the founders and shareholders and will find it. Banks in the region tend to avoid crypto exposure. Bring a clear, written answer: separate legal entity, separate shareholders where possible, no shared funds, systems or data, no crypto in the product, and ideally a distinct brand.

### 2. The regulatory plan

- Proof the BCEAO payment-institution (EP) application is filed, or a dated filing plan.
- BCEAO **agent registration** if Jokko deals with customers before its own licence.
- A legal opinion on the partnership structure.
- Proof that Jokko never holds client funds before its licence: the money sits in the bank's ring-fenced accounts.

### 3. The AML/CFT programme (BCEAO Instruction 001-03-2025, which explicitly covers fintechs)

- AML policy and procedures, a risk map, and KYC tiers by customer type (individual, sole trader, company).
- Real-time screening of customers and transactions against sanctions and PEP lists.
- A named AML compliance officer reporting to the CEO.
- Suspicious-transaction reporting to CENTIF, an annual independent audit and an annual report to the BCEAO.
- Business onboarding (KYB): how Jokko verifies RCCM, NINEA, directors and beneficial owners.

### 4. The outsourcing contract (Instruction 001-01-2024, arts. 36–37)

- Service levels and KPIs, the bank's and the Banking Commission's audit rights, data ownership and IP, exit and business-continuity clauses.
- **30 days' notice to the BCEAO** before outsourcing a critical function or launching a new product.
- **Exclusivity clauses are banned by the BCEAO**, so you can keep a second bank in reserve.

### 5. Security and technology

- **PCI DSS** compliance (or a certified processor that carries it).
- Visa Third-Party Agent and Mastercard Service Provider registration, filed by the bank, with annual due diligence.
- Penetration tests, IT security policy, incident response, business-continuity and disaster-recovery plans.
- Data-protection filings with Senegal's data-protection authority (CDP), including transfers of data abroad to cloud or KYC providers.
- Architecture: core ledger, reconciliation, how the bank's systems and PI-SPI connect.

### 6. The commercial terms they will propose

- A setup fee and a monthly platform fee.
- A **collateral or prefunding deposit** (for card settlement and chargebacks).
- Minimum volumes or minimum monthly fees.
- A split of card interchange revenue.
- Placement of client funds with them, which is their main interest: payroll deposits are cheap funding for a bank.

No Senegalese price list is public, so get at least three quotes. Your leverage: you bring deposits, SME clients and card volume, and exclusivity is banned.

### 7. For the lending partner (SFD)

- A credit policy: eligibility, limits, scoring, collections.
- Proof that the all-in rate stays under 24%, with fees inside it.
- Reporting to the credit bureau (BIC).
- **First-loss cover from Jokko**, typically 5–20% of the portfolio (to negotiate).
- Data-sharing and consumer-protection commitments: no auto-disbursement, no contacting borrowers' phone contacts.

## Questions for counsel before the first bank meeting

1. Is Code de procédure civile art. 381 still in force, and what deduction share applies today?
2. Does an employer-funded earned-wage advance fall under Labour Code art. L.130 ("acomptes") rather than credit rules?
3. Which agent status must Jokko register under to onboard customers for a partner bank or SFD before its own licence?
4. Does the 0.5% transfer tax apply to PI-SPI transfers and to supplier payouts made from a payment account?
5. Is a shared "Jokko" brand with a crypto project acceptable to the BCEAO and to partner banks, and what disclosure is required?
6. How is the civil-servant payroll deduction code (via the Treasury's payroll department) obtained, and by whom?
