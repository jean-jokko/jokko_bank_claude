# Consumer Digital Banking & Wallet Competitors in Francophone West & Central Africa (as of October 2026)

Research date: 2026-10-08. Coverage focus: Côte d'Ivoire (CI), Senegal (SN), Cameroon (CM), with notes on Mali/Burkina/CEMAC. Where a figure predates 2025 it is flagged **[OUTDATED?]**. Where a source is a commercial blog/affiliate site it is flagged **[LOW-RELIABILITY]**.

## Q1. Djamo — profile (CI, SN)

### Takeaway
Djamo is the only scaled independent consumer neobank in UEMOA (~2M users by July 2026). Since September 2025 it has its own BCEAO microfinance licence (via Djamo Finances SA), so it can offer interest-bearing savings (up to 6%) and cash loans (up to 1M FCFA). It is now raising a $40M Series C to expand across francophone West Africa. Its cards are Visa cards issued through a partner bank (Ecobank is named), and its revenue comes from card interchange plus a 2,000 FCFA/month Premium tier.

### Cited Findings
**Basics, history, team**
- Founded in 2020 by Hassan Bourgi (CEO) and Régis Bamba. YC-backed. Positions itself "between mobile money and traditional banking". About 250 staff (April 2025) — [TechCrunch, 3 Apr 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/03/djamo-raises-17m-and-has-1m-users-across-francophone-africa)
- Countries: Côte d'Ivoire (home market) and Senegal (more recent), with other francophone West African markets targeted next — [ITWeb, 8 Jul 2026](https://itweb.africa/article/djamo-targets-francophone-africa-with-40m-raise/nWJad7bNlr47bjO1)
- No dated Senegal launch was found. One blog says "expansion Sénégal 2024" and another says "2025–2026" — [Kolonell [LOW-RELIABILITY]](https://kolonell.com/fr/blog/neobanque-senegal-comparatif-2026). TechCrunch called Senegal "more recently" added as of April 2025 — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/03/djamo-raises-17m-and-has-1m-users-across-francophone-africa)

**Users**
- More than 1M customers across CI and SN in April 2025. Over 55% were previously unbanked, about one third are women, and 25% pay for the Premium tier. Only 5–10% receive their salary in Djamo (the goal is 50%) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/03/djamo-raises-17m-and-has-1m-users-across-francophone-africa)
- About 2 million users as of July 2026 — [ITWeb](https://itweb.africa/article/djamo-targets-francophone-africa-with-40m-raise/nWJad7bNlr47bjO1)
- No active-user figure has been disclosed — gap.

**Licence / partner bank**
- The BCEAO granted a **microfinance licence** to Djamo Finances, announced on 11 Sep 2025 and described as the first BCEAO microfinance licence for a fintech. It enables unrestricted current accounts, savings at up to 6% per year, and cash credit up to 1M FCFA — [Ecofin, 12 Sep 2025](https://www.ecofinagency.com/finance/1209-48632-ivorian-fintech-djamo-breaks-new-ground-with-bceao-microfinance-approval)
- Cards for international and online payments are offered through partner banks. Ecobank is named for the international-payment cards, and a local bank partnership for SME cross-border payments is in progress — [ITWeb](https://itweb.africa/article/djamo-targets-francophone-africa-with-40m-raise/nWJad7bNlr47bjO1)
- TechCrunch reports a "first fintech-issued brokerage licence" in the region, which enables investment products — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/03/djamo-raises-17m-and-has-1m-users-across-francophone-africa). Ecofin says Djamo already offered investment access on the BRVM before the MFI licence — [Ecofin](https://www.ecofinagency.com/finance/1209-48632-ivorian-fintech-djamo-breaks-new-ground-with-bceao-microfinance-approval)
- In Feb 2025, CDC-CI invested CFA 800M in "Djamo's e-money operations" — [Ecofin](https://www.ecofinagency.com/finance/1209-48632-ivorian-fintech-djamo-breaks-new-ground-with-bceao-microfinance-approval). That wording suggests an e-money entity also exists, but no EME licence for Djamo was confirmed (see Gaps).

**Funding & investors**
- Series A of $14M (2022). A $17M round in April 2025, led by Janngo Capital, with SANAD Fund (Finance in Motion), Partech, Oikocredit, Enza Capital and Y Combinator. It was the largest round ever for an Ivorian startup, and the valuation had "doubled" since the last raise (undisclosed) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/03/djamo-raises-17m-and-has-1m-users-across-francophone-africa)
- Total raised: $31M equity plus $5M debt. The company is targeting a $40M Series C (July 2026), with no close reported — [ITWeb](https://itweb.africa/article/djamo-targets-francophone-africa-with-40m-raise/nWJad7bNlr47bjO1). Aggregators disagree: PitchBook says $51.1M and Tracxn says $34.6M — [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/459803-98), [Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/djamo/__X3o91rvbP6txFNn3ZqGWyuQ3r4D6lqwcrxsagAC3pj8)
- Revenue is up 5x since 2022. More than $4.5B has been processed since launch. Revenue comes from merchant fees on online card purchases and the Premium tier — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/03/djamo-raises-17m-and-has-1m-users-across-francophone-africa). The CEO speaks of "entrenching profitability in our home market" — [ITWeb](https://itweb.africa/article/djamo-targets-francophone-africa-with-40m-raise/nWJad7bNlr47bjO1)

**Product features**
- Cards and P2P transfers, savings vaults ("coffres"), investments, salary-linked accounts, a Premium tier, and business tools (bulk payments, payment links, QR) for about 10,000 SMEs. Distribution is a hybrid of app plus offline agents who meet customers in person — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/03/djamo-raises-17m-and-has-1m-users-across-francophone-africa)
- Free IBAN (to receive a salary or make bank transfers). Savings pots: 2 simultaneous on Free, 20 on Premium. Cash loans up to 1,000,000 F in-app with no guarantor, at 0.78% interest plus a 1% processing fee, with more than 30,000 loans in two months — [Erooamba comparison 2026 [LOW-RELIABILITY]](https://erooamba.com/posts/djamo-vs-wave-cote-ivoire-2026)
- Djamo Premium is a Visa Platinum card with Agoda/Booking/IHG discounts and purchase protection on purchases over $100 — [Djamo](https://www.djamo.com/en-ci/learn/djamo-premium-visa-platinum-benefits)
- Business line: a cash-management account, payment collection and an API — [Senego](https://senego.com/senegal-uemoa-la-fintech-djamo-mise-sur-la-banque-digitale-pour-accelerer-linclusion-financiere_1991125.html)
- No crypto: "We are not going to be doing cryptocurrencies like bitcoin". Djamo is discussing a stablecoin cross-border sandbox with the central bank — [ITWeb](https://itweb.africa/article/djamo-targets-francophone-africa-with-40m-raise/nWJad7bNlr47bjO1)

**Pricing (official page, CI)**
- Free plan: 0 F/month. Premium: 2,000 F/month. No fees on payments, top-ups or UEMOA ATM withdrawals. Transfers to Mobile Money or banks cost 1.5% on Free and 0 F on Premium. Withdrawals abroad cost 2% (minimum 2,500 F). Card replacement costs 5,000 F — [Djamo card pricing](https://www.djamo.com/en-ci/learn/the-cheapest-payment-cards)
- Payments outside the UEMOA cost 200 F plus the Visa FX rate. Premium Mobile Money transfers are free only up to 1,000,000 F/month, then 1%, which conflicts with the official "0 F" — [Erooamba [LOW-RELIABILITY]](https://erooamba.com/posts/djamo-vs-wave-cote-ivoire-2026)

**Reputation**
- iOS: 4.6 average from 1,341 reviews (AppFollow). Djamo itself claims 4.7/5. Review themes include high fees that vary month to month and one report of a card deleted with funds on it — [AppFollow](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/djamo/1504072402?country=us), [mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/djamo/1504072402), [Djamo about](https://www.djamo.com/en-ci/about)

### Inferences
- With an MFI licence (savings plus credit) and a bank-sponsored Visa, Djamo is the closest analogue to Jokko and the most direct competitor. Its moat is brand and first-mover status with the young urban salaried and freelance segment, plus the IBAN. Its weakness is dependence on a partner bank for cards and the 1.5% fee on outbound transfers to wallets on the free tier. That fee will look expensive once PI-SPI makes small interoperable transfers free (see Q6).
- Only 5–10% of users receive their salary in Djamo, so it is mostly a *secondary* account. The primary-account relationship (salary domiciliation) is still contestable.

### Gaps
- Djamo's own EME status (whether it holds a BCEAO e-money licence or still runs e-money through a partner) was not confirmed by a primary source.
- No official salary-advance product was found (an earlier product existed according to older press, unverified). There is no active-user figure and no Google Play rating.

## Q2. Wave — profile

### Takeaway
Wave is the price-setter and the most dangerous incumbent. Its model is a free wallet with free deposits and withdrawals, a 1% send fee, free bill pay and a free virtual Visa. It has 23–25M monthly users across about 11 countries (company claim). It holds a BCEAO EME licence (2022) and now owns a commercial bank in CI, Wave Bank Africa (Oct 2025), and is preparing credit, savings and insurance. By value it holds 38.2% of UEMOA mobile money versus Orange's 41.3% (2024).

### Cited Findings
- Founded in 2018 by Drew Durbin and Lincoln Quirk. In April 2022 it received a BCEAO e-money licence, the first non-bank, non-telco fintech in UEMOA. That licence covers e-money, merchant payments, savings, transfers and mobile microcredit without partner banks — [Ecofin](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-finances/2610-49842-wave-launches-commercial-bank-in-cote-d-ivoire)
- **Wave Bank Africa S.A.** was reported on 26 Oct 2025: registered in CI, capital of 20 billion FCFA (about $32M), offering deposits, lending, accounts and payments. Its CEO is Bamba Abdoulaye Katier — [Ecofin](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-finances/2610-49842-wave-launches-commercial-bank-in-cote-d-ivoire)
- Funding: a $200M Series A (2021) at about a $1.7B valuation. No newer valuation was found — [PanAfrican Visions, May 2026](https://panafricanvisions.com/2026/05/how-wave-built-a-23-million-user-fintech-empire-in-africa/). Debt of €117M (reported as $137M), June 2025, led by RMB with BII, Finnfund and Norfund — [Ecofin](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-finances/2610-49842-wave-launches-commercial-bank-in-cote-d-ivoire), [Fintech News Africa](https://fintechnews.africa/45533/senegal/wave-137m-debt-funding/)
- Scale (company claims): more than 23M monthly active users, more than 10M active payers, more than 2M merchants, more than 50k agents and more than 3,000 staff — [PanAfrican Visions](https://panafricanvisions.com/2026/05/how-wave-built-a-23-million-user-fintech-empire-in-africa/). In July 2026 the company claimed more than 25M monthly users, 150,000 active agents and 11 countries — [Financial Afrik, 23 Jul 2026](https://www.financialafrik.com/en/2026/07/23/interview-with-joel-bertrand-ndjodo-country-manager-of-wave-cameroon/). In CI it had more than 20M accounts and about 150k merchants (Mar 2024) — [Ecofin](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-finances/2610-49842-wave-launches-commercial-bank-in-cote-d-ivoire)
- Country list (per one source): Senegal, CI, Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger, The Gambia, Sierra Leone, Cameroon, Uganda and DRC — [PanAfrican Visions](https://panafricanvisions.com/2026/05/how-wave-built-a-23-million-user-fintech-empire-in-africa/). Ecofin says "eight markets". **Conflicting**, so verify Niger, Sierra Leone and DRC.
- 2022: about 15% of staff (close to 300) laid off, mostly in Burkina, Mali and Uganda, with a scale-back to focus on SN and CI — [TechCabal 2022](https://techcabal.com/2022/07/13/senegalese-unicorn-wave-lays-off-15-of-staff/). Wave denied closing those subsidiaries — [Osiris](https://www.osiris.sn/wave-dement-la-fermeture-de-ses-filiales-au-mali-burkina-et-en-ouganda.html)
- **Cameroon (2025)**: entered through Commercial Bank Cameroon (CBC), operating under CBC's licence, which COBAC authorised in June 2025. It offers deposits, withdrawals, P2P and bill pay, and the flat 1% pricing pushed Orange Cameroon to cut fees — [Launch Base Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2025/07/14/fee-wars-begin-in-cameroon-as-unicorn-disruptor-wave-enters-and-telcos-fight-to-hold-ground/). Cameroon pricing: free cash-in, 1% P2P, free withdrawals, free bill pay — [Financial Afrik](https://www.financialafrik.com/en/2026/07/23/interview-with-joel-bertrand-ndjodo-country-manager-of-wave-cameroon/)
- **Mali (Feb 2026)**: the DGCC found that Wave was "selling at a loss" by absorbing Mali's 1% cash-withdrawal levy (in force since 7 Feb 2025) and ordered a tariff revision — [Osiris](https://www.osiris.sn/mali-coup-de-tonnerre-sur-le-mobile-money-wave-sa-rappele-a-l-ordre.html)
- **Market share (BCEAO 2024 data)**: by value, Wave rose from 34.0% to 38.2%, Orange Money fell from 43.3% to 41.3%, MTN fell from 16.3% to 11.7%, Moov went from 2.8% to 2.6%, and Yas from 2.1% to 2.0%. By volume, Orange holds 38.4% and Wave 23% — [Launch Base Africa, Mar 2026](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2026/03/24/the-267bn-crown-wave-edges-closer-to-dethroning-orange-in-west-africa/)
- **Virtual Visa card**: CI version with Orabank (around Nov 2025). Senegal version launched on 23 Jan 2026 with Visa and Ecobank Sénégal after a pilot in CI and SN — [Financial Afrik](https://www.financialafrik.com/2026/01/24/senegal-wave-lance-une-carte-virtuelle-avec-visa-et-ecobank/), [Osiris](https://www.osiris.sn/wave-en-partenariat-avec-visa-et-ecobank-lance-une-carte-virtuelle-pour.html). The card is free, with limits of 500k F per transaction and 2M F per month internationally (CI, early 2025 data) — [Digital Mag CI](https://digitalmag.ci/cote-divoire-la-carte-visa-virtuelle-wave-est-maintenant-accessible/?amp=1). It is **refused by Apple** because Apple excludes prepaid cards in West Africa — [Digital Mag CI](https://digitalmag.ci/paiement-numerique-pourquoi-la-carte-virtuelle-de-wave-ne-marche-pas-sur-apple/?amp=1)
- **Products in preparation (SN, Feb 2026)**: credit, savings and insurance ("simples, responsables et accessibles"), plus BRT/TER e-ticketing — [Ndarinfo/Financial Afrik](https://www.ndarinfo.com/Wave-38-millions-de-comptes-credit-epargne-et-assurance-en-preparation_a42954.html). Auto insurance was reportedly rolled out in CI on 22 Jul 2026 (search snippet only; unverified)
- Wave Pro (a merchant/entrepreneur offer) launched in January 2025 per a blog — [Kolonell [LOW-RELIABILITY]](https://kolonell.com/fr/blog/neobanque-senegal-comparatif-2026)
- CI comparison: Wave has no IBAN, no savings accounts and no loans. The wallet balance cap is 2,000,000 F and the daily transaction cap is 1,000,000 F — [Erooamba [LOW-RELIABILITY]](https://erooamba.com/posts/djamo-vs-wave-cote-ivoire-2026)
- **PI-SPI**: Wave was absent from the BCEAO instant-payment platform at launch and was connected on 30 Sep 2026, the final deadline, in CI and also authorised in SN — [AllAfrica, Oct 2026](https://allafrica.com/stories/202610050727.html), [Launch Base Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2026/03/24/the-267bn-crown-wave-edges-closer-to-dethroning-orange-in-west-africa/)
- Fraud: social-engineering fraud is the main type. In CI, the cybercrime unit (PLCC) handled 1,232 wallet-fraud cases in 2021 **[OUTDATED?]** — [Osiris](https://www.osiris.sn/Cote-d-Ivoire-Wave-expose-les.html). A Dakar case in Dec 2025 involved about 1.76M FCFA stolen from a Wave account — [Seneweb](https://www.seneweb.com/en/news/Justice/scam-identity-theft-how-a-driver-emptied-his-bosss-wave-account_n_481729.html)

### Inferences
- Wave's free card, free bill pay, banking licence and coming credit and savings will erode Djamo-style differentiation within 12–24 months. Competing on price for basic P2P is futile. Wave's 1% P2P fee is now exposed by PI-SPI (free up to 8,000 F/day, at most 0.8% above), so Wave may cut it. **Expect the payments layer to trend toward zero.**
- Wave's gaps today: no IBAN, low balance caps (2M F), a prepaid card only (Apple refuses it), no salary account, and no live interest-bearing savings or credit (as of the latest evidence).

### Gaps
- No current (2025–2026) valuation. Country list inconsistent (8 vs 11). No confirmed launch date or terms for Wave credit or savings. App store rating not retrieved (Google Play fetch failed). Gambia and Uganda status for 2026 unverified.

## Q3. Telco mobile money (Orange Money / Orange Bank Africa, MTN MoMo, Moov Africa Money, Mixx by Yas, Airtel Money)

### Takeaway
Telcos still have the largest bases and agent networks, and they are rapidly copying neobank features: Mastercard cards (Orange 2024, Mixx 2025, Moov CI Dec 2025, MTN CM Dec 2025), super-apps (Orange Max it) and nano-credit (Orange Bank Africa's Tik Tak has lent 640bn FCFA). They are losing value share to Wave. Their typical complaints are tiered and opaque fees, cash-out fees and weak support.

### Cited Findings
**Orange Money / Orange Bank Africa (OBA)**
- Group-wide 2024: 100M registered accounts, 40M monthly actives, 9B transactions worth €164B, about 600k points of sale (CI 66.4k, CM 92.4k), and 340k+ Tik Tak clients in 2024. A Mastercard virtual and physical card linked to Orange Money was signed in late 2024 for 7 countries — [WeAreTech](https://www.wearetech.africa/fr/fils/actualites/telecom/orange-money-9-milliards-de-transactions-pour-191-3-milliards-en-afrique-en-2024)
- OBA launched in CI in July 2020 with NSIA Banque. Savings start from 1 FCFA up to 10M FCFA at 3.5% (rate as of 2020) **[OUTDATED?]** — [Ecofin](https://www.ecofinagency.com/telecom/1806-42761-cote-divoire-orange-bank-disbursed-over-80-million-in-loans-in-10-months), [FrenchWeb](https://www.frenchweb.fr/orange-bank-africa-le-groupe-de-telecoms-lance-sa-banque-mobile-en-cote-divoire/403567)
- Tik Tak: 2M customers, 51% outside Abidjan, more than 500bn FCFA lent over 5 years, loans up to 1M FCFA in seconds (July 2025) — [CIO Mag](https://cio-mag.com/orange-bank-africa-revele-les-coulisses-qui-simplifient-lacces-au-microcredit-tik-tak-depuis-5-ans/)
- OBA passed 3M customers (CI 80%, SN 20%, Senegal branch opened 2024). Tik Tak is used by 37% of active customers, and 640bn FCFA in credit has been granted since July 2020 (Feb 2026) — [AllAfrica](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202602260522.html)
- Max it super-app launched in CI on 5 Jan 2024 (merging My Orange and Orange Money). Motor insurance with Baloon was added in Aug 2026 — [Osiris](https://osiris.sn/Cote-d-Ivoire-Orange-lance.html), [Capmad](https://www.capmad.com/post/orange-plugs-baloons-motor-insurance-into-max-it-super-app-in-cote-divoire)
- In Senegal, Orange removed transfer fees and added a 1% withdrawal fee capped at 5,000 F (per MP Saliou Ndione) — [Launch Base Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2026/03/24/the-267bn-crown-wave-edges-closer-to-dethroning-orange-in-west-africa/). In CI, receiving is free and withdrawal costs 1% (cap 4,500 F) — [Lesafriques 2026](https://lesafriques.com/2026/09/envoyer-argent-cote-ivoire-comparatif-frais-taux-2026/)
- Orange CI group 2025 revenue was 1,197.1bn FCFA (+10.4%), with "very strong competition notably in mobile money" and increased regulatory and tax pressure — [Financial Afrik](https://www.financialafrik.com/2026/02/19/en-2025-le-groupe-orange-cote-divoire-enregistre-une-croissance-a-deux-chiffres-atteignant-ainsi-lensemble-de-ses-objectifs-operationnels-et-financiers)

**MTN MoMo**
- UEMOA value share fell from 16.3% to 11.7% (2023→2024) — [Launch Base Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2026/03/24/the-267bn-crown-wave-edges-closer-to-dethroning-orange-in-west-africa/)
- MoMoKash (savings and nano-credit) in CI per an MTN case study (undated) — [MTN](https://www.mtn.com/case-study/unlocking-digital-credit-through-mobile-money/)
- Cameroon: a virtual MoMo prepaid Mastercard with Access Bank Cameroon (Dec 2025) — [Business in Cameroon](https://www.businessincameroon.com/finance/2412-15553-mtn-launches-mastercard-backed-virtual-momo-prepaid-card-in-cameroon). Withdrawals cost 1% via the app versus 1.5% plus 4 F tax via USSD (July 2025) — [Digital Business Africa](https://www.digitalbusiness.africa/cameroun-les-tarifs-de-retrait-de-mtn-mobile-money-momo-en-2025/)

**Moov Africa Money**
- About 2.6% UEMOA value share — [Launch Base Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2026/03/24/the-267bn-crown-wave-edges-closer-to-dethroning-orange-in-west-africa/). On 5 Dec 2025, Moov CI launched a new Moov Money app and a Moov Money Mastercard (physical and virtual) — [Fratmat](https://www.fratmat.info/article/2638627/economie/mobile-money-un-tournant-majeur-pour-la-digitalisation-financiere-en-cote-divoire), [Digital Mag](https://digitalmag.ci/moov-africa-devoile-la-moovmoney-card-pour-les-paiements/)

**Mixx by Yas (formerly Free Money, Senegal; Axian group)**
- Rebranded from Free Money in early 2024. A Mixx Mastercard prepaid card launched in June 2025 with Ecobank Sénégal. Nano-credit has reached about 30,000 beneficiaries since 2024 (figures inconsistent) — [Osiris](https://www.osiris.sn/mixxby-yas-lance-une-carte-prepayee-pour-renforcer-l-inclusion-financiere-au.html), [Osiris](https://osiris.sn/mixx-senegal-une-premiere-annee-sous-le-signe-de-l-inclusion-et-de-l-innovation.html). UEMOA value share is about 2.0% — [Launch Base Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2026/03/24/the-267bn-crown-wave-edges-closer-to-dethroning-orange-in-west-africa/)

**Airtel Money (Chad, DRC, Gabon, Congo-B, Niger, Madagascar)**
- London IPO priced at a £5.3bn valuation, with admission expected on 14 Oct 2026. Revenue was $1.346bn for FY to March 2026, and 44.6M users in FY to March 2025. Francophone revenue grew 22% — [Daba Finance](https://dabafinance.com/en/news/airtel-money-sets-ipo-price-at-1-96-valuing-business-at-5-3-billion-2026-10-01)

**Cameroon/CEMAC context**
- Cameroon applies a 0.2% e-money transfer tax (since 2022) plus a 4 FCFA per-transaction levy (since Jan 2025). The IMF says small-transaction costs rose about 20% — [Business in Cameroon](https://www.businessincameroon.com/finance/1412-15500-cameroon-mobile-money-tax-sharply-raises-transaction-costs-imf-warns), [Ecomatin](https://ecomatin.net/virements-retraits-paiements-par-carte-transferts-de-titresau-cameroun-le-prelevement-de-4-fcfa-suscite-debat)
- Camtel is preparing "Blue Money" for 2026 — [Camer.be](https://www.camer.be/93232/11:1/camtel-prepare-son-entree-dans-le-mobile-money-et-bouscule-mtn-et-orange-au-cameroun-cameroon.html)

### Inferences
- Orange Bank Africa (Tik Tak) is the real incumbent for consumer digital credit in CI and SN: 640bn FCFA lent, with distribution through Orange Money. A new neobank cannot out-distribute it for nano-loans. It should differentiate on larger, longer, salary-backed credit and transparency.
- Cards are now commoditised: every telco and Wave offers a prepaid Visa or Mastercard. Differentiation must come from *acceptance quality* (debit rather than prepaid, Apple, Google and recurring-subscription acceptance), limits and FX pricing.

### Gaps
- Country-level 2025–26 active users for Orange Money CI and SN, MTN CI and CM, and Moov CI. Current OBA savings rate. Current official fee grids (only secondary sources found). Airtel francophone user split.

## Q4. Banks' digital offerings and the YUP failure

### Takeaway
Traditional banks have mobile apps and some wallet tie-ins, such as the Ecobank Xpress Account and bank-to-Wave/OM transfers. But fully remote retail onboarding is still rare. SGCI only integrated ONECI real-time ID verification in Nov 2025. Banks increasingly act as *BIN sponsors / card issuers* for fintechs (Ecobank for Djamo, Wave SN, Mixx; Orabank for Wave CI; Access Bank CM for MTN; CBC for Wave CM). Société Générale's YUP (2017–2022, 7 countries) shut down because it never found a viable model against cheaper, more lightly regulated telco and fintech wallets.

### Cited Findings
- **YUP**: Société Générale's mobile money, launched 2017 (some sources say late 2018), in Burkina, Cameroon, CI, Guinea, Ghana, Madagascar and Senegal. Closure announced on 1 Mar 2022. Reasons given: it "failed to create a viable model" despite efforts across 7 countries, the market was "oversaturated" with new competitors, regulators raised requirements on banks while non-bank providers "are not subject to this regulation", and the group refocused on core banking. ### Inferences
- **Lessons from YUP:** (1) a bank-owned wallet carrying bank-grade compliance costs without a bank-grade product (credit, savings, IBAN) cannot win on price against Wave and telcos; (2) a me-too P2P wallet with no distribution edge fails; (3) shutting down abruptly damages trust with B2B salary clients. For Jokko, value must come from banking products (credit, savings, salary), not P2P fees, and the operation needs a sustainable unit economics path before scaling across 7 countries.
- Banks are better partners (BIN sponsor, deposit custody, credit balance sheet) than competitors for a consumer neobank. Several, including Ecobank, Orabank, CBC and Access, are clearly willing.

### Gaps
- No 2025–26 specifics found for NSIA, Coris Money, BOA, UBA, Orabank, BICICI or Bridge Bank consumer apps (features, fees, ratings). Note that BICICI was taken over by Ivorian state/investors after BNP's 2022 exit (from training knowledge, not verified here). The Ecobank Xpress Account's current terms in CI and SN were not verified.

## Q6. Regulatory and pricing environment reshaping competition (2025–2027)

### Takeaway
BCEAO's PI-SPI instant-payment system becomes **mandatory for interoperable e-money transfers from 2 Nov 2026**. Interoperable P2P transfers will be free up to 8,000 FCFA per day per user per provider, and at most 0.8% above that. Receiving is always free. Cross-UEMOA transfers follow from 1 Jun 2027. Cash-out, merchant, bill and credit fees remain unregulated. This compresses P2P revenue for everyone, including Wave's 1% and Djamo's 1.5%.

### Cited Findings
- PI-SPI launched on 30 Sep 2025. From 2 Nov 2026 interoperable e-money transactions within UEMOA must go via PI-SPI. National transfers are free if the daily cumulative total is ≤8,000 FCFA per user per participant, and may be charged 0–0.8% (excluding tax) above that. Receiving is free. From 1 Jun 2027 the same rules apply to cross-border transfers within UEMOA. The scope covers bank/MFI↔EME/PI and EME↔EME — [Droit Médias Finance, citing BCEAO communiqué of 2 Oct 2026](https://droitmediasfinance.com/index.php/actualites/droit-tech-fintech/1402-uemoa-la-bceao-rend-pi-spi-obligatoire-et-elargit-la-gratuite-des-transferts-de-monnaie-electronique-des-le-2-novembre-2026)
- The 8,000 F threshold covers about 75% of UEMOA e-money transactions. Agent withdrawals, merchant payments, bills, salary disbursement and credit remain freely priced — [Senego](https://senego.com/bceao-les-petits-transferts-gratuits-mais-les-retraits-restent-hors-plafond_2009714.html), [AllAfrica](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202610040114.html)
- Wave is not legally required to align its *internal* (on-us) transfers, which do not cross BCEAO infrastructure — [Osiris](https://www.osiris.sn/pi-spi-le-piege-tarifaire-de-wave.html)
- Market structure: 248M mobile money accounts in UEMOA, but only 76.8M (31%) active in 90 days (down from 45% in 2020). About 88% of wallet deposits are withdrawn as cash. Merchant transactions are 23.3% of volume. Registered merchants rose from 1.75M to 3.7M. CI plus SN make up about 65% of value — [Launch Base Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2026/03/24/the-267bn-crown-wave-edges-closer-to-dethroning-orange-in-west-africa/)
- Senegal: a proposed transfer tax (TTA) is under discussion in 2026 — [Ndarinfo](https://www.ndarinfo.com/Wave-38-millions-de-comptes-credit-epargne-et-assurance-en-preparation_a42954.html). Mali: a 1% withdrawal levy since Feb 2025 — [Osiris](https://www.osiris.sn/mali-coup-de-tonnerre-sur-le-mobile-money-wave-sa-rappele-a-l-ordre.html)
- Financial inclusion: CI rose from 41% (2017) to 58% (2024) — [ITWeb, citing World Bank](https://itweb.africa/article/djamo-targets-francophone-africa-with-40m-raise/nWJad7bNlr47bjO1). A secondary source says 51% with 28M mobile money accounts and 13M active (conflicting) — [SBS](https://sbs-software.com/insights/cote-divoire-banking-sector/). Senegal mobile money ownership is about 67% (up from 6% in 2014) — [Ezo/Findex 2025](https://www.ezo.app/blog/2025-global-findex-financial-inclusion-and-mobile-tech-in-sub-saharan-africa)

### Inferences
- From Nov 2026 a new entrant can reach every wallet and bank account in UEMOA cheaply via PI-SPI. This *lowers* the network-effect moat of Wave and Orange for a newcomer: Jokko users can pay anyone. P2P stops being a revenue line, so revenue must come from interchange, subscriptions, credit and float/savings spread.
- High cash-out (88%) and low activity (31%) mean the "spend digitally" loop is weak. Products that keep money in-account (interest-bearing savings, card spend, bill pay, salary) address the core problem.

### Gaps
- Whether the 0.8% fee applies to the full amount or only the excess above 8,000 F. Operator fee responses after 2 Nov 2026 (not yet published).

## Q7. Customer pain points with existing services

### Takeaway
The pain points reported most often are fraud and account takeover (social engineering), costs that are hard to predict, cash-out fees and agent liquidity, weak complaint handling, and online and international card acceptance (prepaid cards refused by Apple and others). Lack of savings and credit access is also cited. Hard data (review analytics, surveys) was sparse, so this section relies on press and operator statements.

### Cited Findings
- Wave's own Cameroon country manager lists the market's unmet needs: "costs that are hard to anticipate, complex user experience, irregular service availability and frequent stockouts at service points, weak support for customer complaints", plus fraud risks — [Financial Afrik, Jul 2026](https://www.financialafrik.com/en/2026/07/23/interview-with-joel-bertrand-ndjodo-country-manager-of-wave-cameroon/)
- Fraud: Wave accounts were emptied (more than 30 accounts in days in one locality in 2023), and police complaints rose in Dakar **[OUTDATED? 2021–23]**. Social engineering is the main vector — [Osiris](https://www.osiris.sn/En-quelques-jours-plus-de-30.html), [Osiris](https://www.osiris.sn/Arnaques-via-Wave-Les-plaintes.html), [Osiris CI](https://www.osiris.sn/Cote-d-Ivoire-Wave-expose-les.html). Rumours of "account blocks without reason" were denied by Wave Senegal — [Osiris](https://osiris.sn/Blocages-de-comptes-sans-raison.html)
- Card acceptance: the Wave CI virtual Visa is refused by Apple because Apple excludes prepaid cards in West Africa — [Digital Mag CI](https://digitalmag.ci/paiement-numerique-pourquoi-la-carte-virtuelle-de-wave-ne-marche-pas-sur-apple/?amp=1). Netflix says declines can stem from international, online or recurring-payment blocks on the card — [Netflix Help](https://help.netflix.com/fr/node/2065)
- Djamo reviews mention month-to-month fee variation and a card deleted with funds — [mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/djamo/1504072402). Premium markets "no fees on rejected transactions" and "priority customer service", which implies these are known pain points — [Erooamba [LOW-RELIABILITY]](https://erooamba.com/posts/djamo-vs-wave-cote-ivoire-2026)
- Credit and savings gap: access to credit and savings remains limited despite rising inclusion in CI — [ITWeb](https://itweb.africa/article/djamo-targets-francophone-africa-with-40m-raise/nWJad7bNlr47bjO1). Wave has no savings or loans yet in CI — [Erooamba [LOW-RELIABILITY]](https://erooamba.com/posts/djamo-vs-wave-cote-ivoire-2026)
- Cameroon: taxes raised small-transaction costs by about 20% and larger tiers by up to 400% (IMF), which prompted user campaigns — [Business in Cameroon](https://www.businessincameroon.com/finance/1412-15500-cameroon-mobile-money-tax-sharply-raises-transaction-costs-imf-warns), [ITWeb](https://itweb.africa/content/G98YdMLY18zqX2PD)
- Barriers in sub-Saharan Africa (Findex): lack of money is the top barrier (59% of the unbanked), then cost, distance and documentation (KYC). Phone access is less binding in West Africa — [Ezo/Findex 2025](https://www.ezo.app/blog/2025-global-findex-financial-inclusion-and-mobile-tech-in-sub-saharan-africa), [World Bank Findex 2021](https://www.worldbank.org/en/publication/globalfindex/brief/data-from-the-global-findex-2021-progress-and-obstacles)
- Interoperability: no direct Wave→Orange Money transfer in CI, so users must withdraw then redeposit (pre-PI-SPI mandate) — [Erooamba [LOW-RELIABILITY]](https://erooamba.com/posts/djamo-vs-wave-cote-ivoire-2026)
- Low balance caps: Wave's maximum balance is 2M F and daily limit 1M F — [Erooamba [LOW-RELIABILITY]](https://erooamba.com/posts/djamo-vs-wave-cote-ivoire-2026)

### Inferences
- An offering built around fewer card declines plus security (anti-scam UX, transaction confirmations, rapid human support in French and local languages) plus transparent flat pricing directly targets the top three complaints.

### Gaps
- No systematic Google Play review mining was possible (the Play Store fetch failed). No quantitative survey on frozen accounts, KYC friction or failed transactions specific to CI, SN or CM was found. Recommend scraping Play reviews (Djamo, Wave, Max it, MoMo, Moov Money) as a follow-up.

### Inferences (gaps and opportunities for Jokko)
1. **The "primary account" gap.** Nobody combines the zero-fee wallet simplicity of Wave with a full bank account (IBAN, salary domiciliation, debit card, overdraft). Djamo is closest but mostly secondary (5–10% salaried). Opportunity: salary-domiciliation partnerships with employers, paired with an *earned wage access / salary advance* product. No live salary-advance product was confirmed at any competitor.
2. **Cards that actually work internationally.** Every competitor card is prepaid, with Apple refusing Wave's. A true *debit* (or credit) Visa or Mastercard via a bank BIN, tokenised for Apple Pay and Google Pay, with transparent FX (beating "Visa rate + 200 F") and a USD sub-wallet for subscriptions (Netflix, ChatGPT, AliExpress), is a clear wedge.
3. **Savings with yield and goals.** Only Djamo (up to 6%) and OBA (old 3.5% rate) offer interest. Wave is planning savings. A transparent, daily-accrual, high-yield vault (for example backed by BRVM T-bills or UMOA-Titres) could lead the market before Wave launches.
4. **Credit beyond nano-loans.** Tik Tak and Djamo cap at 1M F. Salary-backed instalment loans, BNPL at merchants (none found among competitors) and credit-builder products are open.
5. **Diaspora.** Remittance apps pay into wallets but no player offers a diaspora-openable account with family cards, bill pay and savings. The BNDE/Kopar project is unconfirmed. LemFi is entering the francophone corridors.
6. **Trust and support.** Fraud, opaque fees, weak complaint handling and agent stockouts are recurring. Differentiators: anti-scam UX, instant human support, a fee calculator, and no fees on failed transactions.
7. **PI-SPI tailwind.** From 2 Nov 2026 a newcomer can reach every wallet and bank. P2P becomes a free feature rather than a moat, which lowers Wave and Orange network lock-in. Monetisation must come from interchange, subscription, credit and savings spread.
8. **Geography.** Cameroon/CEMAC is less contested by fintechs (Wave only entered in 2025 under CBC's licence; Djamo is absent) but hit by e-money taxes (0.2% + 4 F). Card spend and in-account savings avoid much of that tax burden. Mali and Burkina add political and regulatory risk.
9. **Lessons from failure (YUP).** Do not build a me-too P2P wallet. Have a credit and savings revenue model before expanding to several countries. Plan B2B client transitions carefully.
