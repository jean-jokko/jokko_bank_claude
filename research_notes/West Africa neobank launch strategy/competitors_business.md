# Business/SME Banking & Payments Competitive Landscape: Francophone West & Central Africa (as of Oct 2026)

Scope note: These notes cover B2B and SME accounts, payouts, payroll, collections, expense cards and SME lending in WAEMU (UEMOA) and CEMAC, plus playbooks from other African SME neobanks. Research date: 8 Oct 2026. Several company figures are self-reported marketing claims, and some sources are vendor blogs (notably Kolonell, a Dakar agency that sells payment integrations). These are flagged where they appear. Items dated before 2025 are flagged as possibly outdated.

---

## 1. Francophone B2B fintech players: profiles (license, countries, clients, funding, features, pricing)

### Takeaway
The francophone B2B field has two parts. The first is a small set of business "accounts" or treasury tools: Julaya (the clear B2B leader), Djamo Business and Wave Business. The second is a larger set of payment aggregators and orchestrators (CinetPay, PayDunya, Bictorys, Naboopay, InTouch, Hub2, Bizao, FedaPay, Kkiapay, Paystack, Flutterwave, plus Maviance/Smobilpay, CamPay and NotchPay in Cameroon). The BCEAO's 2024 payment-institution regime forced every player to get licensed in 2025. As of 28 Feb 2026, 31 payment institutions (EPs) were authorised. No one yet offers a full "Qonto-style" stack: an account with its own RIB, expense cards, approvals, accounting, tax payments and credit together.

### Inferences
- Julaya is the only player whose published product resembles a Qonto-style business account: sub-accounts, approvals, accounting exports, payroll, its own RIB and a prepaid card, across four WAEMU markets. Its main weaknesses are no public pricing, no visible credit product despite a 2022 promise, and no CEMAC presence.
- Djamo Business is the freemium threat in Abidjan and Dakar (zero account fees, employee cards). But it is built as an extension of a consumer app, without visible multi-user approvals, and with low ceilings for informal businesses (10M FCFA per month).
- Wave sets the price anchor for payouts in Senegal and CI, at 1% sender-pays and free to recipients. Any B2B payouts product must match or beat about 1% on Wave rails or bundle more value. A licensed Wave Bank would be a serious threat in SME deposits and credit if approved.
- The aggregators (CinetPay, PayDunya, Naboopay, Paystack and others) mostly sell collections at 1.5–3.5% and are not account or treasury products. They are partners or rails as much as competitors.
- Hub2 and InTouch are potential infrastructure partners (multi-operator payout routing) for a new entrant rather than direct competitors.

### Gaps
- No public pricing was found for Julaya, Djamo Business transfer fees (the pricing page returned 404), Hub2, InTouch or Bizao.
- No 2026 client counts were verified for Julaya, Bictorys or CinetPay.
- The licence status of Bizao, Hub2, Kkiapay and FedaPay under the BCEAO regime was not confirmed, and the full 31-name BCEAO list (Feb 2026) was not retrieved.
- COBAC/BEAC licence status was not confirmed for Maviance, CamPay or NotchPay.
- Not found or not researched: Sycapay details (only "SYCA SA" appears as an EP), Afrikpay, Ejara, Ozé, Kalpa, "Zeta". Orange Money Business and merchant fees were not retrieved.
- The PayDunya–Peach Payments acquisition rests on a single aggregator source.

## 2. Banks' SME offerings and SME lenders (incumbents and digital lenders)

### Takeaway
Banks remain the only providers of full current accounts, cheques and trade finance. Account opening is slow and in-person, and fees are 10–25K FCFA per month. On credit, the most scaled digital SME lender is Orange Bank Africa (Tik Tak nano-loans backed by IFC guarantees), with Wave Bank pending. Development finance institutions keep funding bank SME credit lines, which means the credit gap is large and is being addressed mainly through banks.

### Cited Findings
- **CI account opening:** requires the RCCM, IDU, DFE, statutes, management minutes, ID and headquarters proof. At least one signatory must be physically present in Abidjan, and **100% online opening is not possible**. Expect 3–10 days after the in-person appointment (IDU validation takes 3–5 days). Company registration documents themselves can take up to 12 weeks. Banks cited: BNI (flexible for new SMEs), SGCI (large accounts), Ecobank (import/export), plus BOA and Coris. This comes from a consulting firm's promotional page dated July 2026. — [ACF-CI](https://acf-ci.com/ouvrir-compte-bancaire-professionnel-en-cote-divoire/)
- **Senegal bank fees:** UBA Sénégal business account at **25,000 FCFA per month for SA/SARL/PME and 10,000 FCFA per month for TPE/sole traders** (comparison site, unverified). FBNBank Senegal requires statutes, RCCM, legal publication notice, minutes appointing directors, IDs and proofs of residence. — [Comparek.sn](https://comparek.sn/banques/produits/compte-entreprise); [FBNBank Sénégal](https://www.fbnbanksenegal.com/les-services-bancaires-personnels/comptes-courants/compte-entreprise/)
- **Ecobank CI:** 80% of its transactions are digital, with digital deposits growing 15–20% (May 2026), and it has 10,000 "Express" agent points. It runs SME working-capital lines and a gender bond (IFC/ALCB) for about 1,200 loans to women-led SMEs. — [Digital Mag CI](https://digitalmag.ci/digitalisation-80-des-operations-de-transaction-decobank-cote-divoire-passent-par-le-digital/?amp=1); [Abidjan.net](https://news.abidjan.net/articles/626431/economie-solutions-pour-la-croissance-des-pme-ecobank-cote-divoire-propose-ses-offres)
- **Société Générale and IFC (2026):** IFC partnership to expand MSME finance. In Senegal, fewer than a quarter of MSMEs have bank loans and the MSME finance gap is about 7.2% of GDP. — [IFC press release 2026](https://www.ifc.org/en/pressroom/2026/ifc-partners-with-soci-t-g-n-rale-to-expand-access-to-finance-for-msmes-create-job)
- **NSIA Banque CI:** BII €30M facility for MSMEs and women's entrepreneurship. — [BII](https://www.bii.co.uk/en/news-insight/news/british-international-investment-and-nsia-group-strengthen-partnership-with-e30-million-facility-to-nsia-banque-cote-divoire-to-support-msmes-and-womens-entrepreneurship/). A local guide recommends NSIA and Bridge Bank for responsiveness and Coris for trade-oriented SMEs (blog, low reliability). — [EroambaHub](https://erooamba.com/posts/meilleures-banques-cote-ivoire-2026)
- **Orange Bank Africa (Tik Tak):** more than 747bn FCFA of loans issued to more than 3.6M customers since 2020 (about 80% CI, 20% Senegal; Senegal branch opened Feb 2025). In Feb 2026, IFC announced a FCFA 17bn (~$30.6M) guarantee covering up to 50% of a portfolio of up to $60M of SME working-capital loans distributed through Tik Tak in CI and Senegal. There is a separate guarantee with Côte d'Ivoire's SME guarantee agency (SGPME) for up to FCFA 17bn. — [Agence Ecofin](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-finances/2607-57726-cote-d-ivoire-s-sme-agency-orange-bank-sign-guarantee-agreements-to-expand-sme-lending); [Agence Ecofin, Feb 2026](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-finances/0302-52548-ifc-to-provide-30-6m-guarantee-for-orange-bank-digital-lending-in-cote-d-ivoire-senegal)
- **Factoring and leasing:** UMOA authorities are positioning factoring and leasing as "strategic pillars" of SME/SMI financing (Apr 2026). — [Financial Afrik EN](https://www.financialafrik.com/en/2026/04/20/umoa-making-factoring-and-leasing-strategic-pillars-of-sme-smi-financing/)
- **BCEAO SME refinancing scheme** (2015/2016): SMEs are defined as turnover ≤ 1bn FCFA. Bank uptake was weak (2020 consultation). — [BCEAO](https://www.bceao.int/fr/content/presentation-du-dispositif-de-soutien-au-financement-des-pmepmi); [Financial Afrik 2020](https://www.financialafrik.com/2020/08/12/visioconference-regionale-sur-le-dispositif-de-financement-des-pme-zone-uemoa/)

### Inferences
- Incumbent banks compete on trust, cheques, trade finance and credit, not on user experience. Mandatory in-person KYC (as at Djamo and the banks) is a sector-wide norm, not a single competitor's weakness.
- DFI guarantee capital (IFC, SGPME, BII) is actively looking for digital SME lending channels. A B2B neobank with transaction data could partner with a licensed bank or MFI and tap those guarantees rather than lend from its own balance sheet early on.
- Orange Bank's Tik Tak shows that short-tenor, data-driven nano and working-capital loans scale in CI. That is evidence of demand, and also a strong incumbent.

### Gaps
- SME account fee schedules for SGCI, Ecobank, Coris, Bridge Bank and Afriland were not retrieved, and no minimum-balance data was found.
- Advans, Baobab, Fenix, Kubuka, invoice factoring fintechs and Afriland SME offerings were not researched for lack of time and calls.
- No Orange Bank Africa outstanding-portfolio (encours) or NPL data was found.

## 3. Pan-African SME neobank playbooks (Moniepoint, Kuda Business, Lenco, Brass, Paystack MFB, TymeBank)

### Takeaway
The winning African SME playbook is **payments/acceptance first, then the operating account, then credit underwritten on proprietary transaction data**, all under a deposit-taking licence. Moniepoint (POS to MFB bank to $720M of SME loans, profitable, >$1B valuation) and TymeBank (retail-partner distribution plus an acquired merchant cash advance lender) are the proof points. Standalone SME account plays without a payments moat or credit engine (Brass) failed or were absorbed.

### Cited Findings
- **Moniepoint scale:** 6M+ businesses and 16M individuals. Its POS terminals handle "8 in 10 in-person payments in Nigeria". About ₦30tn (~$21.6B) was processed in 2025 (one source); other sources cite ₦412tn and 14B transactions, a conflict that remains unresolved. About 3,300 staff. — [LaunchBase Africa, Jan 2026](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2026/01/29/from-payments-to-credit-nigerian-fintech-moniepoint-deploys-720m-to-corner-shops-and-supermarkets); [Techeconomy](https://techeconomy.ng/tag/moniepoint-report/)
- **Moniepoint credit:** more than ₦1tn (~$720M) of credit to SMEs over the past year, underwritten on its own transaction data plus Moniebook (bookkeeping SaaS) records. Average borrower growth is 36%. Top sectors are provision stores (15%) and building materials (8%). The impact report says about 75% of borrowers were getting formal credit for the first time. It holds a national MFB licence and is a Visa/Mastercard processor and acquirer through TeamApt. — [LaunchBase Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2026/01/29/from-payments-to-credit-nigerian-fintech-moniepoint-deploys-720m-to-corner-shops-and-supermarkets); [Leadership NG](https://leadership.ng/?p=651785)
- **Moniepoint funding:** a $110M Series C in 2024 (crossed $1B valuation), plus $90M in Oct 2025 led by DPI with IFC, LeapFrog, Google's Africa Investment Fund and Visa, about $200M total for the round. Claims profitability (unaudited). — [Techpoint](https://techpoint.africa/news/moniepoint-series-c-extension/); [People of Color in Tech](https://peopleofcolorintech.com/articles/moniepoint-nigerias-billion-dollar-fintech-raises-an-additional-90m-from-visa-google-and-others/)
- TechCabal estimated Moniepoint's payments revenue at about ₦1.24tn (0.30% take rate) and loan income at about ₦180bn. These are **estimates, not reported figures**. — [TechCabal (via search summary)](https://techcabal.com/?p=180023)
- **Kuda Business** (Nigeria, MFB licence): focuses on invoicing, collections and payments for freelancers and MSMEs. About 100K businesses (mid-2024). Offers account-number-based "POS" without hardware. Kuda processed ₦14.3tn in H1 2025 (₦8.5tn retail, the rest business). — [ThisDay](https://thisdaylive.com/index.php/2024/07/01/empowering-nigerias-msmes); [IT Edge News](https://www.itedgenews.africa/kuda-hits-₦14-3-trillion-transactions-unveils-ai-powered-expansion-plans-and-multicurrency-wallet/); [MSME Africa](https://msmeafricaonline.com/kuda-launches-virtual-and-physical-pos-solutions-to-support-african-smes/)
- **Lenco** (Nigeria, YC W22): a free business current account opened digitally in minutes, with no maintenance fees or minimum balance. Offers multi-currency, expense management, virtual cards, invoices and **bulk payouts of up to 5,000 payments**. Seed stage. — [Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/lenco); [VC4A](https://vc4a.com/ventures/lenco/)
- **Brass** (Nigeria, founded 2020): offered SME accounts, payroll, expense and cash-flow tools. A liquidity crisis from late 2023 brought withdrawal delays. It was rescued by a Paystack-led consortium in May 2024 and announced in June 2026 that it would stop operating independently, with customers migrating to Paystack MFB by 31 Jul 2026. — [Brand Spur](https://brandspurng.com/2026/06/30/brass-to-shut-down-as-independent-fintech-customers-move-to-paystack-microfinance-bank/); [Techeconomy](https://techeconomy.ng/brass-migrates-paystack-mfb-shutdown-2026)
- **Paystack MFB** (Jan 2026): bought Ladder MFB to take deposits and lend, **starting with business lending** (working capital, merchant cash advance, overdrafts). It uses live payment data from 300K+ Nigerian businesses. Nigeria's SME financing gap is about $32B. Paystack was earlier fined ₦250M by the CBN for operating beyond its licence (Zap). — [Techpoint](https://techpoint.africa/news/paystack-acquires-ladder-bank/); [Techloy](https://www.techloy.com/stripe-owned-paystack-enters-banking-expanding-beyond-payments/)
- **TymeBank / GoTyme:** distribution through retailers (Pick n Pay, Boxer, TFG). It acquired merchant cash advance lender Retail Capital (R1.5bn, Dec 2022), has disbursed more than $600M to 80,000+ SMEs in South Africa, and saw 30% SME book growth in 2023. Profitable since Dec 2023 (South Africa). In the Philippines it lends to PayMongo merchants (up to PHP 500K, repaid from daily sales) and TikTok Shop sellers (1.5–2.5% per month). It enters some markets (Indonesia, Hong Kong) as an MCA lender without a bank licence. — [Fintech News PH](https://fintechnews.ph/61058/digital-banking-news-philippines/tyme-groups-digital-bank-achieves-profitability-in-record-time/); [BusinessTech](https://businesstech.co.za/news/banking/803626/tymebank-now-making-sustainable-profits/); [Daba Finance](https://dabafinance.com/en/news/south-africas-tymebank-plans-indonesia-expansion-in-asia-push); [Fintech News PH (TikTok)](https://fintechnews.ph/67755/lending/gotyme-bank-partners-tiktok-shop-for-msme-seller-loans/)

### Inferences
- The lesson that transfers to WAEMU/CEMAC: own a **high-frequency payment flow** (collections, payouts or acceptance) to get data and float, then add credit underwritten on that data. In francophone Africa that flow is mobile money, not cards and POS. The equivalent of Moniepoint's POS is likely **multi-operator MoMo collections plus payouts**, the space Julaya, Wave and the aggregators occupy.
- Brass shows the risk of an SME account-only model without balance-sheet strength. Liquidity and safeguarding of customer funds are existential, which supports the case for a bank or e-money issuer partner, or for Jokko's own licence.
- Tyme's model of entering as a merchant cash advance lender first, through platforms that hold sales data, is a capital-light route to credit while a licence is pending.

### Gaps
- No audited unit economics (CAC, ARPU, loss rates) were found for Moniepoint, Kuda Business or Tyme's SME lines.
- Sparkle, Anchor (BaaS), Pesapal and M-Pesa Fuliza/Fuliza Biashara were not researched.

## 4. SME pain points in WAEMU/CEMAC (evidence-based)

### Takeaway
The documented pain points are: (1) slow, in-person, paperwork-heavy bank onboarding; (2) cheque dependence for large B2B value; (3) fragmented mobile money across operators, now partly addressed by BCEAO's PI-SPI instant rail since Sep 2025; (4) international card payments for ads and SaaS failing on local cards; (5) a large credit gap (more than half of MSMEs credit-constrained); and (6) new compliance load: mandatory e-invoicing (FNE) in CI from 2025–26, Senegal e-invoicing legislated, and social-security and tax payments moving online.

### Cited Findings
- **Onboarding:** see Section 2. In CI, in-person presence is mandatory with no fully online opening, an IDU check, and a 3–10 day timeline after the appointment. — [ACF-CI](https://acf-ci.com/ouvrir-compte-bancaire-professionnel-en-cote-divoire/). Djamo Business also requires the legal representative to be present. — [Djamo](https://www.djamo.com/en-ci/business-account)
- **Cheques:** cheques made up 71.7% of SICA-UEMOA clearing value in 2025 but only 24.6% of volume. Transfers were 74.1% of volume. SICA processed 31.2M operations worth FCFA 88.5tn in 2025. — [LaunchBase Africa, Jul 2026](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2026/07/24/71-of-waemu-transaction-value-still-flows-through-cheques-despite-bceaos-new-instant-payment-rail/); [Financial Afrik, Jul 2026](https://www.financialafrik.com/2026/07/28/bceao-1-336-453-milliards-de-fcfa-ont-transite-par-les-systemes-de-paiement-de-lumoa-en-2025-record/). In 2024, cheques were 71.77% of value and transfers 20.30%. — [Seneweb](https://seneweb.com/en/news/Afrique/sica-uemoa-un-marche-interbancaire-dynamique-mais-fidele-aux-methodes-traditionnelles_n_465192.html)
- **Interoperability (PI-SPI):** BCEAO launched PI-SPI on 30 Sep 2025. It runs 24/7 on ISO 20022, with phone-number-based transfers across banks, e-money wallets and MFIs, and payments are irrevocable once validated. By 31 Dec 2025, 74 institutions were connected (58 banks, 7 e-money institutions, 9 MFIs). No volume data has been published, and real traction is described as "a 2026 story". — [LaunchBase Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2026/07/24/71-of-waemu-transaction-value-still-flows-through-cheques-despite-bceaos-new-instant-payment-rail/)
- **Multi-operator fragmentation:** there are more than 150 independent MoMo operators across Africa (Hub2's framing). Hub2 claims an average transfer success rate of about 60% for competitors against its own 97.8% (company claim). — [Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/hub2_io)
- **Payroll pain:** a Djamo Business testimonial says paying 15 employees "took half a day" before Djamo. A Julaya client says it now pays 500 salaries in under 5 minutes. These are testimonials. — [Djamo](https://www.djamo.com/en-ci/business-account); [julaya.co](https://julaya.co)
- **International payments (ads, SaaS):** most GIM-UEMOA cards and some local Visa/Mastercard cards are not enabled for international online payments by default or fail 3-D Secure. Meta pauses ad accounts after repeated declines. Prepaid neobank BINs are often blocked. Vendor-estimated decline rates are 10–20% for local neobank cards and 3–8% for premium USD fintech cards (unverified). — [Kolonell, Aug 2026](https://kolonell.com/fr/blog/cartes-virtuelles-paiement-en-ligne-afrique-francophone-2026); [WorldFirst](https://www.worldfirst.com/af/blog/virtual-cards/virtual-cards-for-facebook-ads-africa/). In CEMAC, a BEAC rule reportedly limits online payments abroad to 1M FCFA per month per user. — [Cameroon Tribune (via search summary)](https://cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/58940/fr.html/details_2). There were 8.0M bank cards in UEMOA at end-2023, 20.8% of them prepaid. — [Financial Afrik](https://financialafrik.com/2024/11/20/dans-luemoa-la-monetique-a-le-vent-en-poupe)
- **Credit gap:**
  - Côte d'Ivoire: the MSME gap is FCFA 1,368.2bn (~$2.4B), 55% of MSMEs lack formal credit, and the figure is 78% for women-led businesses (IFC 2022). — [Agence Ecofin](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-finances/1007-57261-cote-d-ivoire-new-28-6-million-facility-to-expand-credit-access-for-msmes)
  - Senegal: the gap is 7.2% of GDP and fewer than 25% of MSMEs have bank loans (IFC 2026). — [IFC](https://www.ifc.org/en/pressroom/2026/ifc-partners-with-soci-t-g-n-rale-to-expand-access-to-finance-for-msmes-create-job)
  - 63% (Senegal) and 57% (CI) of MSMEs are credit-constrained. — [FinDev Gateway](https://www.findevgateway.org/data/msme-finance-gap)
  - Only about 13% of outstanding loans in the BCEAO zone went to small businesses (2018–2020). — [ITWeb](https://itweb.africa/content/nWJadvbebZ9qbjO1)
  - Burkina Faso and Mali have an MSME credit gap above $3B combined. — [DFC disclosure](https://ewsdata.rightsindevelopment.org/projects/DFC-2021-STICHTINGCORDA)
- **E-invoicing (CI FNE):** the Facture Normalisée Électronique is mandatory under the Annexe fiscale 2025 and Arrêté 0337/MFB/DGI (9 May 2025). It is issued through an ERP or API, the DGI platform or app, or certified terminals (TERNE), with RNE receipts for B2C. Transitions ended on 1 and 11 Dec 2025 for real-regime companies and micro-enterprises. Entreprenant-regime taxpayers had until 13 Feb 2026 for B2B and B2G. Sources conflict on exact dates and a three-month extension. — [Sage](https://www.sage.com/fr-ma/blog/la-fne-en-cote-divoire-guide-complet-sage-cote-divoire/); [APA News](https://fr.apanews.net/governance/rci-facture-electronique-cloture-des-inscriptions-fin-janvier/); [Edicom](https://edicomgroup.com/fr/blog/obligation-facturation-electronique-cote-d-ivoire)
- **Senegal e-invoicing:** legislated in Loi de finances 2025 (loi n°2025-02 of 28 Dec 2024) for VAT-registered taxpayers. The rollout timeline is **unclear or contradictory** (vendor blogs cite Jan/Jul 2026 thresholds or 2027–28). Penalty reports also conflict. — [Sage](https://www.sage.com/fr-ma/blog/senegal-les-nouvelles-obligations-de-la-facturation-electronique/); [Thomson Reuters/Pagero](https://europe.thomsonreuters.com/pl/zgodnosc/regulatory-updates/senegal)
- **Social security:** CI's e-CNPS allows online declaration and payment, but no evidence was found that mobile money is accepted. In Senegal, IPRES/CSS launched the "Ndamli" one-stop declaration and payment platform regionally (Matam, 2026). The traditional route is paying at a cashier by cheque or transfer. — [Digital Mag CI](https://www.digitalmag.ci/digitalisation-e-cnps-faites-vos-declarations-et-paiements-de-cotisation-en-ligne/); [AllAfrica, May 2026](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202605210542.html); [Senegal eRegulations](https://senegal.eregulations.org/procedure/103/64/step/213?l=fr)

### Inferences
- Cheques persist because large B2B and B2G payments need irrevocability, paper evidence and bank accounts. A B2B neobank that offers a RIB, PI-SPI-based instant transfers and payment proofs could replace cheque flows. However, PI-SPI also commoditises cross-operator transfers over time, which erodes the "we connect all operators" moat.
- The FNE mandate creates a forced digitisation moment in CI. An invoicing module certified or interfaced with the FNE API, tied to collections and reconciliation, is a strong acquisition hook.
- Paying for international ads and SaaS is a sharp, recurring and monetisable pain (FX plus card fees) for digital SMEs, e-commerce sellers and agencies. It requires a card BIN that platforms accept and an approved FX setup under UEMOA regulation 09/2010.

### Gaps
- No quantitative survey was found on reconciliation time, payroll costs or cash-handling costs for WAEMU SMEs.
- No primary BCEAO or BEAC text on corporate card FX limits was retrieved.
- Cheque usage specifically by SMEs (as opposed to all clearing) and minimum-balance norms were not found.

## 5. SME segment size and willingness to pay

### Takeaway
The addressable base is large but mostly informal. Côte d'Ivoire's enterprise census had counted about 1.2M formal and informal enterprises at 75% completion, about 600K of them in Abidjan, and roughly 26K companies are formally created each year. Willingness-to-pay benchmarks are thin. Bank account fees run 10–25K FCFA per month in Senegal, fintechs are moving to zero subscription (Djamo), and revenue comes from transaction fees of about 1% (Wave payouts) and 1.5–3.5% (collections).

### Inferences
- Formal companies (RCCM-registered, banked or bankable, about 25K new per year in CI) are the realistic early target for a full business account. The 1M+ informal enterprises are better served through a lighter "pro wallet" tier with lower limits, similar to Djamo's informal tier.

A tiered plan (free tier with transaction fees, plus paid tiers of about 5–25K FCFA per month adding approvals, multi-user, cards and accounting/FNE) is plausible, but this is not validated by survey evidence.

### Inferences (gaps and opportunities for a B2B neobank)
1. **Start B2B with the "money-out" job: payouts, payroll and supplier payments with approvals.** This is the best-evidenced pain (half-day payroll, cash, multiple operators), and Julaya proves companies will adopt it. Differentiate on multi-user controls, PI-SPI plus multi-operator routing (via Hub2/InTouch-type partners), and payslips plus CNPS/IPRES-ready exports. Price against Wave's 1%, for example ≤1% or a flat fee per payout, with volume tiers.
2. **Pair it with an FNE-compliant invoicing, collections and reconciliation module (CI first).** The FNE mandate became effective 2025–Feb 2026 and is forcing SMEs onto API-based invoicing. Combining invoice, payment link, MoMo and PI-SPI collection, and automatic reconciliation is a wedge no competitor documents. Senegal follows once DGID timelines firm up.
3. **International expense cards for ads and SaaS** are a sharp, frequent, monetisable pain. They need a BIN accepted by Meta and Google and an FX set-up compliant with UEMOA regulation 09/2010. In CEMAC, the reported 1M FCFA per month limit on online payments abroad constrains this.
4. **Credit second, data-led and capital-light.** Follow the Moniepoint, Paystack MFB and Tyme playbook: underwrite on in-platform cash flows. Start with invoice financing or factoring (UMOA is prioritising it), payroll advances and merchant cash advances through a bank or MFI partner, tapping DFI guarantees (IFC, SGPME). Orange Bank Tik Tak shows the demand and is the main digital competitor.
5. **Replace cheques for mid-size B2B flows**: own RIB, instant PI-SPI transfers, payment proofs and approval workflows. Cheques still carry about 72% of clearing value.
6. **Onboarding as a differentiator**: same-day digital KYB, while staying within the in-person requirement banks and Djamo still apply. Offer a tiered informal "pro" account with limits, similar to Djamo's 2M/10M FCFA tier, and a formal-company tier.
7. **Risks:**
   - Wave Bank, if licensed, could bundle free accounts, 1% payouts and credit.
   - Djamo's zero-fee business account sets the subscription price anchor at zero.
   - PI-SPI commoditises interoperability.
   - Licensing is mandatory: a BCEAO EP or EME, or a COBAC licence in CEMAC.
   - Brass shows how liquidity failure destroys SME trust.
8. **Geography:** CI first (largest base, FNE, Julaya and Djamo compete there), then Senegal (Wave price pressure). Cameroon/CEMAC is less contested in B2B accounts but has tighter FX and licensing regimes. It is a possible second-wave market where only aggregators (Maviance, CamPay, NotchPay, Flutterwave) are present.

### Gaps

- No SME willingness-to-pay survey exists in the sources. Subscription price points above are inferences, not evidence.
- Customer counts are mostly self-reported and sometimes inconsistent (Julaya 10K vs 25K; Djamo 5K vs 10K).
