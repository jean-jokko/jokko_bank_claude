## 1. Macro fundamentals, political/security risk, debt and CEMAC reserve/devaluation risk

### Takeaway
CEMAC is growing slowly (about 2.6% in 2025, about 3% forecast for 2026) and its FX reserves are thin. They stood at 4.2 months of imports in 2025, below the 5-month CEMAC benchmark, while WAEMU had 7.8 months. Gabon and Congo carry debt of about 80–97% of GDP. Officially there is no devaluation on the table, but Citi publicly called devaluation "the core of the CEMAC story" in May 2026. The DRC is the fastest-growing market of the group: 5.6% growth forecast for 2026, inflation of about 2.5%, an IMF ECF programme on track and USD 8.8bn of reserves. Its main risks are the eastern conflict and political unrest in Kinshasa.

### Inferences
- CEMAC FX risk is structural rather than imminent. The BEAC is tightening (rate hike in Dec 2025, higher repatriation rules from 2027), and that usually comes with tighter enforcement of FX controls, not looser. Any 2026–2028 business plan for a CEMAC neobank should include a devaluation stress scenario. In such a scenario, multi-currency/EUR-savings demand would rise sharply while regulators restrict it further.
- Gabon and Congo have sovereign stress (debt above 80% of GDP, arrears, no IMF programme yet). That stress spreads to banks through state arrears to suppliers and government securities, which matters for banking partners and for corporate/SME customers who depend on public contracts.
- On macro grounds alone, the DRC currently looks more stable than CEMAC: high growth, low inflation, an IMF programme on track and rising reserves. Its risks are political and security-related, and its currency regime is shifting (de-dollarization).

## 2. Financial inclusion and digital readiness (Findex 2025, internet, phones, data costs)

### Takeaway
On the 2025 Findex (survey fielded in 2024), Gabon (68%), Cameroon (61%) and Congo (56%) have account ownership close to Côte d'Ivoire (58%) and below Senegal (76%). The DRC (39%) and Chad (21%) are far behind. Internet penetration is highest in Gabon (72%) and lowest in Chad and CAR (12–13%). Data is cheap in the DRC and Congo (US$0.5–0.7/GB) and expensive in Gabon and Chad (US$4.09/GB).

### Inferences
- Addressable "digitally reachable" population is approximated here as internet users. Cameroon (12.6m) ≈ Côte d'Ivoire (13.4m) > Senegal (11.5m). The DRC (34.7m) is the largest in absolute terms. Gabon, Congo and EG together have only about 5.5m internet users.
- Gabon pairs the highest urbanization (91.6%), internet (72%) and income levels in the group. That makes it the best "premium/test" market, though it is very small, and its data costs are high (US$4.09/GB), so the app must be data-light.
- Chad and CAR are not viable for an app-first neobank in 2026–2027 (internet ≤13%, Findex ≤21% for Chad).

### Inferences
- Cameroon has about 1.03 mobile money accounts per inhabitant (30.9m accounts vs 30.1m people). The other five CEMAC states have about 20.3m accounts for about 37.7m people (≈ 0.54 per capita). So outside Cameroon, mobile-money adoption is roughly half as deep, which means more white space but also less mature digital behaviour.
- Cameroon already has a price war: Orange has free P2P via Max it, and Wave is pushing low fees. A neobank cannot win on P2P transfer price. It must differentiate on things wallets do poorly: savings and interest, cards (virtual Visa/Mastercard), salary domiciliation, SME tools, credit and multi-currency.
- Wave's CEMAC model (operating through a partner bank licence under COBAC) is a working precedent for how a foreign fintech can enter CEMAC without its own EME licence on day one.
- In the DRC, the market is fragmented among three strong wallets, with no dominant one and Orange growing fastest. USD-denominated wallets and Visa Pay linkages already exist, so a neobank's multi-currency feature competes with wallets that already hold USD.

## 5. FX regulation (Règlement 02/18) and impact on cards, FX accounts and online payments — the multi-currency question

### Takeaway
CEMAC rules forbid residents from holding foreign-currency accounts except with BEAC authorization. Card use outside CEMAC is capped at 5m FCFA (≈ €7,622) per person per trip, and online/remote payments abroad at 1m FCFA (≈ €1,524) per person per month, with justification above those limits. A multi-currency feature for residents is therefore largely blocked in CEMAC, and the BEAC is tightening rather than loosening in 2026. In the DRC, the economy is about 80–90% USD-denominated and USD accounts are normal, which makes multi-currency a core feature. But the BCC will ban foreign-currency cash transactions from 9 Apr 2027 to push de-dollarization.

### Cited Findings
- Règlement n°02/18/CEMAC/UMAC/CM, Article 43: residents may not open foreign-currency accounts in CEMAC, except resident legal entities authorized by the BEAC. The same prohibition-by-default applies to accounts abroad. Article 45 prohibits FX withdrawals from a resident's FX account to cover local needs. Implementing rules are in BEAC Instruction n°005/GR/2019. — [Règlement 02/18 (droit-afrique)](https://www.droit-afrique.com/uploads/CEMAC-Reglement-2018-02-changes.pdf); [Investir au Cameroun](https://www.investiraucameroun.com/banque/2803-12392-la-beac-durcit-les-conditions-douverture-dun-compte-en-devises-hors-et-dans-la-cemac); [Deloitte](https://blog.avocats.deloitte.fr/cemac-nouvelle-reglementation-changes/)
- BEAC instruction of 10 June 2019 on cards outside CEMAC (debit, credit and prepaid cards):
  - Payments and withdrawals abroad are free up to 5m FCFA (≈ €7,622) per person per trip, with justification beyond that.
  - Remote/online payments are free up to 1m FCFA (≈ €1,524) per person per month.
  - Above those limits, transactions must be justified to the bank or issuer (within 30 days per most sources; Cameroun24 says 90).
  - Issuers report monthly, and the BEAC and COBAC carry out inspections.
  - Sources: [Agence Ecofin, 2019](https://www.agenceecofin.com/finances-publiques/2806-67370-la-beac-plafonne-les-retraits-et-paiements-par-carte-bancaire-hors-de-la-cemac); [Cameroun24](https://cameroun24.net/article/50640-Lutilisation_des_cartes_bancaires_hors_de_la_Cemac.html); [Gabonreview guide](https://www.gabonreview.com/cartes-de-paiement-le-guide-dutilisation-hors-cemac-de-la-beac/)
- BEAC lettre-circulaire n°004/GR/2022 (22 Jun 2022) restated the 5m FCFA per trip cap and required online service imports above 1m FCFA per month to be cleared within 30 days. — [UBA Cameroon note on LC 004/GR/2022](https://www.ubacameroon.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/8/2022/08/LETTRE-CIRCULAIRE-BEAC-N%C2%B0004.GR_.2022-vf.pdf)
- No 2025–2026 change to the card caps was found. Recent BEAC moves are infrastructure upgrades, not liberalization:
  - ISO 20022 (Instruction 001/GR/2025, effective 17 Nov 2025)
  - SYSTAC 2 clearing live since 3 Aug 2026
  - CEMAC interoperable QR-code regulation n°02/26 (8 Apr 2026, launched 29 Jul 2026)
  - IBAN reform for payment and e-money accounts
  - Sources: [BEAC Instruction 001/GR/2025](https://www.beac.int/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Instruction-N%C2%B0001-GR-2025_norme-ISO-20022.pdf); [Financial Afrik, Aug 2026](https://www.financialafrik.com/2026/08/14/cemac-avec-systac2-la-beac-engage-la-modernisation-des-paiements/); [Notre Afrik](https://notreafrik.com/cemac-qr-code-paiement-interoperable/); [Droit Médias Finance](https://droitmediasfinance.com/index.php/actualites/droit-tech-fintech/1120-cemac-paytech-la-beac-appelle-a-une-reforme-des-international-bank-account-number-iban-sur-les-comptes-de-paiement-et-de-monnaie-electronique)
- The BEAC is tightening FX: extractive-sector repatriation rises from 35% to 50% (2027) and 70% (2028). Earlier, the BEAC censured credit institutions for violating FX retrocession rules. — [Finance Week](https://financeweek.net/blog/cemac-citi-preconise-une-devaluation-du-franc-cfa-apres-loffensive-de-la-beac-sur-le-rapatriement-des-devises); [Droit Médias Finance](https://www.droitmediasfinance.com/index.php/actualites/droit-monetaire/758-cemac)
- Prepaid and fintech cards in Cameroon are issued by BEAC-licensed e-money institutions (commercial source; to be verified). — [Lefisk.cm](https://lefisk.cm/blog/carte-bancaire-cameroun-virtuelle-physique-comparatif)
- DRC dollarization:
  - 90.9% of bank deposits were in foreign currency at end-Feb 2025 (BCC note, 11 Apr 2025)
  - The BCC governor says about 80% of transactions are in foreign currency
  - In 2025, about US$10bn of USD banknotes were imported, but only about US$1.5bn ended up in bank deposits
  - On 9 Apr 2026 the BCC's monetary policy committee gave the BCC sole authority to import foreign banknotes and banned foreign-currency cash transactions from 9 Apr 2027
  - Sources: [Bankable, May 2026](https://bankable.africa/en/news/1105-2907-dr-congo-central-bank-85-of-10-billion-in-imported-cash-exited-banking-system-in-2025); [MediaCongo](https://www.mediacongo.net/article-actualite-162561_fin_du_dollar_cash_en_rdc_un_v_u_de_souverainete_et_d_efficacite_de_la_politique_monetaire_mais_sous_des_contraintes.html); [US State Dept ICS 2025](https://www.state.gov/reports/2025-investment-climate-statements/democratic-republic-of-the-congo); [Ecofin Agency](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-finances/0207-57012-dr-congo-becomes-africa-s-live-test-for-dollar-settled-mobile-money)
- Research shows the CDF share in POS, ATS and mobile-money payments rose after the 2024 reforms. — [HAL, 2025](https://hal.science/hal-05229895v1/document)

### Inferences
- For CEMAC, a "multi-currency" product can realistically mean (a) XAF accounts with an international virtual card, within the 1m FCFA per month online and 5m FCFA per trip caps; (b) inbound international receipts into XAF; and (c) cross-CEMAC/WAEMU payments in CFA. Holding EUR/USD balances for residents would need case-by-case BEAC authorization, which is unlikely for retail.
- The online-spend cap of about €1,524 per month and the justification burden (plus repeated bank refusals in practice) are a real pain point for freelancers, students and e-commerce buyers. A well-built virtual card with automated cap tracking and justification upload could be a strong feature, even within the rules.
- In the DRC, USD is the default, so USD/CDF dual-currency accounts are table stakes, not a differentiator. The 2027 ban on USD cash transactions could push USD cash holders into digital USD/CDF accounts, which is a potential acquisition tailwind for a digital player that offers easy USD-to-account and CDF conversion. The policy direction (de-dollarization) is a regulatory risk for USD-heavy products, though.

### Gaps
- No 2026 primary BEAC text confirming the current card caps was found. The caps should be confirmed with a CEMAC issuing bank or on beac.int (Instruction n°008/GR/2019 and any amendments).
- How caps apply to e-money (mobile-wallet) virtual cards specifically was not established.
- BCC rules on USD e-money wallets and whether the April 2027 cash ban affects USD e-money were not found.

## 6. Formal employment and payroll channels

### Takeaway
Public payrolls are bank-paid in Cameroon (since 2019) and Gabon (mandatory since 2014). Cameroon pays 306,159 active civil servants plus 160,285 pensioners (May 2026), with a 2025 wage bill of 1,753.5bn FCFA (≈ €2.67bn). Salary domiciliation is therefore already captured by banks. A neobank would need payroll partnerships or an employer channel.

### Cited Findings
- Cameroon:
  - May 2026: 466,444 people on the State payroll (306,159 active agents and 160,285 pensioners), 13.1% fewer than in March 2023, after the AIGLES payroll system went live on 1 Jan 2025
  - 2025 wage bill: 1,753.5bn FCFA (≈ €2.67bn; +15.1%); 2026 budget: 1,626.2bn FCFA (≈ €2.48bn)
  - Sources: [Ecomatin](https://ecomatin.net/cameroun-les-effectifs-de-letat-chutent-de-13-mais-la-masse-salariale-bondit-de-39); [Tchadinfos, Jan 2026](https://tchadinfos.com/2026/01/15/cameroun-la-masse-salariale-2025-en-forte-hausse-depassant-largement-les-previsions/)
- Cameroon civil servants and pensioners have been paid by individual transfer into their bank or MFI accounts via SYSTAC since 2019. No 2025–2026 confirmation was found. — [Camer.be](https://www.camer.be/76328/11:1/cameroun-soldes-et-pensions-les-virements-desormais-personnalises-cameroon.html)
- Gabon:
  - Public salaries have been paid only by bank transfer since 25 Aug 2014 (staff in towns without banks exempted)
  - BGFIBank, Ecobank, Orabank, UBA, UGB and BICIG were selected to bank civil servants
  - A November 2023 directive told all civil servants and retirees to open bank accounts
  - April 2025 back-pay went by transfer to active agents and by cash voucher to retirees
  - CaPay enables mobile-money payroll for unbanked rural workers
  - Sources: [Journal du Cameroun](https://fr.journalducameroun.com/gabon-le-paiement-des-salaires-des-agents-publics-se-fera-uniquement-par-virement-bancaire/); [Gabonreview](https://www.gabonreview.com/bicig-mise-en-place-dun-dispositif-special-de-bancarisation-des-agents-de-letat/); [WeAreTech](https://www.wearetech.africa/fr/fils/solutions/au-gabon-la-plateforme-capay-facilite-le-paiement-de-masse)
- CEMAC unemployment is about 10%, and population growth about 3% a year. — [World Bank Barometer 2026](https://www.worldbank.org/en/region/afr/publication/economic-barometer-for-the-central-african-economic-and-monetary-community-summer-2026)

### Inferences
- Civil servants are a natural early segment for premium accounts (salary advances, savings), but they are locked into banks through payroll domiciliation. A neobank needs a bank partner that can receive State payroll, or must target private-sector and SME payroll (bulk payments) instead.
- Gabon's state wage arrears and pension arrears (35bn FCFA, ≈ €53m, still owed for back-pay) and Congo's arrears point to cash-flow stress among public employees. That means demand for salary-advance credit, but also credit risk.

### Gaps
- Oil-sector employment and payroll channels in Gabon, Congo and EG were not found.
- Formal private-sector employment counts (CNPS/CNSS) by country were not found.
- Congo, Chad and DRC civil-service payroll channels were not found. The DRC is known to have used bank payroll ("bancarisation de la paie"), but no 2025–26 source was retrieved.

## 7. SMEs: formal counts, informality, business creation, payment behaviour

### Takeaway
Cameroon is the only CEMAC country with good data: about 430,000–444,000 formal enterprises, 99% of them small, about 21,000 new SMEs a year via CFCE one-stop shops, and about 3.7m informal actors. SMEs face effective borrowing rates of about 23%.

### Cited Findings
- Cameroon:
  - RGE3 (2023 census): 430,011 enterprises, 99% small structures, high informality
  - 2024: 444,302 enterprises, 443,524 of them SMEs (+12.8%)
  - Informal sector: about 3.7m actors (2024)
  - Business creation via CFCE: 19,651 (2023), 21,132 (2024), about 21,000 (2025), 42% of 2025 founders under 35
  - CFCE creation cost is 41,500 FCFA (≈ €63), down from 482,000 FCFA before
  - Sources: [Ecomatin](https://ecomatin.net/cameroun-les-cinq-lecons-du-recensement-general-des-entreprises); [Journal du Cameroun](https://fr.journalducameroun.com/cameroun-voici-les-chiffres-des-pme-en-2024/); [Ecomatin](https://ecomatin.net/entrepreneuriat-pres-de-21-000-nouvelles-pme-creees-en-2025-au-cameroun); [Journal du Cameroun](https://fr.journalducameroun.com/entrepreneuriat-les-jeunes-a-lorigine-de-42-des-pme-creees-au-cameroun-en-2025/)
- SMEs are more than 80% of CEMAC's economic fabric. SME TEG was 23.3% (Q2 2025), against 8.77% for large firms. — [Gabon Media Time](https://gabonmediatime.com/cemac-les-pme-penalisees-par-des-couts-de-credit-de-plus-en-plus-eleves/)
- Diool (B2B payments, Cameroon) raised about 2bn FCFA (≈ €3.0m) in early 2021. — [Cameroon CEO](https://cameroonceo.com/2023/01/12/7-fintechs-a-suivre-au-cameroun-en-2023/)

### Inferences
- Cameroon's roughly 440k formal SMEs, plus about 21k new ones a year, are the largest formal-SME base in CEMAC. That makes it the natural SME-neobank market in the region. The 3.7m informal actors are reachable through mobile-money-linked merchant and QR tools, especially with the new CEMAC interoperable QR standard (2026).

### Gaps
- Formal SME counts and informality rates for Gabon, Congo, Chad, EG and the DRC were not found.
- SME payment behaviour (share paid by mobile money vs cash vs bank) was not found.

## 8. Remittances and diaspora

### Takeaway
Officially recorded remittances are large for the DRC (about US$3.3bn, 4.9% of GDP) and moderate for Cameroon (about US$787m, 1.6% of GDP). CEMAC diaspora inflows into mobile wallets reached 1,354bn FCFA (≈ €2.06bn) in 2024 (+77%), about 60% of it from the EU and 20% from North America. Senegal (US$3.27bn, 10.6% of GDP) is the WAEMU benchmark.

### Cited Findings
- World Bank remittances (2023/2024): Cameroon US$787m (1.6% of GDP), DRC US$3,298m (4.9%), Congo US$44m (0.3%), Gabon US$18.5m (0.1%), Côte d'Ivoire US$1,042m (1.3%), Senegal US$3,267m (10.6%). Chad, CAR and EG are not listed. — [Wikipedia, citing World Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_remittances_received)
- BEAC 2024: diaspora transfers received directly into CEMAC mobile money accounts reached 1,354bn FCFA (≈ €2.06bn), +77% from 763.81bn FCFA (≈ €1.16bn) in 2023. About 804bn FCFA (≈ €1.23bn, about 60%) came from the EU, 275bn FCFA (≈ €419m, about 20.3%) from North America, and 21.8bn FCFA (≈ €33m) from West Africa. — [Financial Afrik, May 2026](https://www.financialafrik.com/2026/05/01/cemac-en-2024-les-transferts-de-fonds-de-la-diaspora-via-mobile-money-en-hausse-de-77/)
  - Conflicting report: Agence Ecofin says the value of diaspora transfers via mobile money fell 11.9% to 756.7bn FCFA while the number of operations rose 42.6%. The two articles may measure different flows. — [Agence Ecofin](https://www.agenceecofin.com/actualites-finance/2904-137975-transferts-de-la-diaspora-plus-de-1-000-milliards-fcfa-recus-via-mobile-money-dans-la-cemac-en-2024)
- DRC remittance costs are close to 8% of the amount sent on average (World Bank, as cited). — [Ecofin Agency](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-finances/0207-57012-dr-congo-becomes-africa-s-live-test-for-dollar-settled-mobile-money)

### Inferences
- Inbound remittances are allowed under CEMAC FX rules (Wave/CBC's authorization includes inbound international transfers). A neobank could combine an inbound EUR→XAF corridor with diaspora-funded accounts (e.g. a France-based parent funding a child's account in Douala).
- Gabon and Congo receive negligible remittances. Their opportunity is domestic income, not diaspora flows.
- For the DRC, the roughly 8% remittance cost plus a Belgian/French/US diaspora are a strong wedge for low-cost USD inbound transfers.

### Gaps
- Diaspora sizes (France, Belgium, US) by country were not found.
- Cameroon remittance corridor costs were not found.
- The World Bank figures above come from a Wikipedia table and should be verified on data.worldbank.org (BX.TRF.PWKR.CD.DT).

## 10. DRC specifics (summary)

### Takeaway
The DRC is the largest market in scope: 114m people, 34.7m internet users, about 34m active mobile money accounts and US$123bn GDP (2026). It is USD-dominated (about 91% of deposits), cheap for data (US$0.52/GB), growing at about 5.6% under an IMF programme, and under-banked (Findex 39%). Its risks are the eastern war (Goma under M23, banks shut), Kinshasa protests over the constitution, and a de-dollarization drive including a ban on USD cash transactions from April 2027.

### Cited Findings
- Population 114m (Oct 2025), 48.8% urban, median age 15.8, internet 30.5%. — [DataReportal DRC](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-democratic-republic-of-the-congo)
- About 34m active mobile money accounts (Q1 2026). M-Pesa had 15.4m active users, Airtel 11.1m and Orange 7.7m (2025). — [Bankable](https://bankable.africa/en/digital/1305-2927-with-194-8-million-in-2025-revenue-airtel-money-closes-in-on-m-pesa-in-drc); [Ouragan](http://www.ouragan.cd/2026/05/m-pesa-vs-airtel-money-ces-millions-us-que-larptc-feint-dignorer)
- In Goma, the banking district is shut, ATMs are out of service, and residents rely on mobile money, paying up to 3.5% per withdrawal (Jan 2026). — [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2026/1/29/banks-shut-futures-uncertain-one-year-after-m23-rebels-seized-drcs-goma)
- Rawbank (US$6.82bn assets) and Equity BCDC (US$5.075bn) lead. 65.2% of deposits are in Kinshasa. — see Section 4 sources
- Dollarization figures, the April 2027 USD cash ban and the 13.5% policy rate — see Sections 1 and 5 sources

### Inferences
- Launch geography would be Kinshasa first (most deposits) and Lubumbashi second (mining payroll and businesses). The East (Goma/Bukavu) is out of scope while under M23 control, though mobile-money demand there is acute.
- Multi-currency (USD/CDF) is essential and well matched to demand. Regulatory direction favours CDF, so the product should make CDF conversion seamless rather than USD-only.

### Gaps
- BCC licensing categories for EMEs and digital banks, and the timelines involved, were not researched.
- Lubumbashi-specific data was not found.
- Kinshasa smartphone penetration was not found.

## 11. Gabon and Congo-Brazzaville specifics (summary)

### Takeaway
Gabon is a small (2.6m), highly urban (92%), connected (72% internet), high-income market (about US$9,900 GDP per capita) with a near-monopoly incumbent bank (BGFI) and the most expensive credit in CEMAC. It is attractive per user but tiny, and the sovereign is stressed (debt about 80%+, CCC-). Congo-Brazzaville is mid-income (US$2,554), urban (70%) and has cheap data (US$0.68/GB), but carries very high debt (about 97% of GDP) and arrears, and has just re-elected its long-standing president.

### Inferences
- Gabon is a good "second CEMAC market" once a Cameroon licence or passport exists (the CEMAC single licence eases cross-border branching). On its own, it has too few customers to justify a standalone launch. Public-spending cuts in 2026 will squeeze the civil-servant segment.
- Congo-Brazzaville has decent digital fundamentals but the weakest sovereign balance sheet. It ranks below Gabon.

### Gaps

**Suggested scoring model (indicative, for the founder to adjust).** Scores run 1–5, where 5 is most attractive. Weights:
- Market size (addressable digital adults and GDP): 20%
- Income/ARPU potential: 15%
- Digital and financial readiness: 15%
- Macro/FX stability: 15%
- Regulatory and FX friction for a multi-currency neobank: 10%
- Competitive white space: 10%
- Political/security risk: 10%
- Ecosystem/partners/talent: 5%

Rationale highlights:
- **Cameroon (3.20).** Largest CEMAC base (30.9m mobile money accounts, 12.6m internet users, about 440k formal SMEs, 19 banks) and the most fintech activity. It loses points for CEMAC FX controls, which constrain multi-currency, and for the contested 2025 election and succession risk. Competition is intense in P2P (MTN/Orange/Wave), but neobank-grade products (cards, savings, SME credit) are absent.
- **DRC (2.95).** Largest raw market (114m people, US$123bn GDP, about 34m active wallets, US$3.3bn remittances) with the best multi-currency fit (USD economy), cheap data and strong macro under the IMF programme. Low income per head, Findex at 39%, Kinshasa-concentrated deposits and the war in the East pull it down. It could outrank Cameroon for a founder who prioritizes multi-currency and scale over risk.
- **Gabon (2.70).** Best per-capita economics and digital readiness, plus a weak incumbent (BGFI concentration, 21% average loan rates). But only 2.6m people and severe sovereign stress (CCC-, about 80%+ debt, no IMF programme yet). Best treated as a follow-on market under a CEMAC licence.
- **Congo-Brazzaville (2.35).** Decent urban and digital profile, but about 97% debt-to-GDP, arrears and political stasis.
- **Chad, EG, CAR (≤ 2.0).** Not recommended for an app-first launch before 2028. Chad and CAR lack digital penetration, and EG is in recession with very few mobile connections.

**Sequencing implied:** a WAEMU base (CI/Senegal), then Cameroon as the CEMAC beachhead (partner-bank or EME route, as Wave/CBC did), then Gabon and Congo via CEMAC passporting. The DRC would be a separate, USD-first track that needs its own BCC licence and Kinshasa-first go-to-market.

### Gaps
- The scoring weights and scores are analyst judgements based on the cited data, not sourced facts.
- The WAEMU rows lack debt, growth, mobile money and data-price data in this thread and should be completed from the WAEMU research notes.
- Licensing cost and time (BEAC EME licence, COBAC payment institution, BCC EME) were not researched and could change the ranking materially.
